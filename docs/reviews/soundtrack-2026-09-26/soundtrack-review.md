# Rogue Dawn / Emblem Rogue — soundtrack review

Reviewed September 26, 2026. PR #134 snapshot `00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe`; directional world bible from PR #119 snapshot `7d5c1af8f849139352a79715a5d1c30f84774e3b`.

**Scope and confidence.** This covers all **49 compositions**, including compositions predating #134, the login arrangement, all **27 calm battle arrangements**, **10 boss enrage arrangements**, and the finale's additive hum: **88 delivered music files**. Three specialist reviews covered field composition, boss composition, and audio engineering; I reviewed the menus, maps, result themes, lore relationships, and consolidated the findings. Actual note/form/orchestration code and runtime selection were inspected, and all 88 MP3s were measured. This is **score and signal analysis, not a firsthand listening review**. I cannot certify subjective timbre, perceived masking, emotional impact, or codec transparency without an audio audition. Recommendations about those qualities below are hypotheses to test, not claims that I heard a defect. Short ceremony stingers and SFX are outside the main-track review.

The local working checkout was older than these PRs. Read-only snapshots were used; no game source or audio was changed, and no review was posted to GitHub. Bar references include written intros; timestamps are approximate first-pass score times, not times from the loop marker.

## Overall assessment

The strongest asset is the soundtrack's dramatic identity. A rising Thread, a mechanical imperial drill, an unanswered religious cadence, and progressively removed notes give the music a story of its own. The best newer pieces also have distinct compositional mechanisms: the fog hides part of a tune, the caravan moves over a bass that avoids its tonic, the castle reinterprets a held pitch, and the Entity's finale restores earlier music collectively. Preserve these ideas.

The next pass should focus on **foreground clarity, melodic development, and contrast over time**. Several older battle/boss arrangements use repeated eight-bar statements, octave doubling, a continuous motor, a bass reinforcement stack, then another larger return. All are useful devices, but repeated across a long run they can make different scenes share an expressive ceiling. More instruments and more loudness are unlikely to be the best general improvement. Make one musical argument audible at a time, then change who carries it.

The audio measurements support that priority: levels are already consistent and individual files have sensible peak headroom. Fidelity work should protect the source masters and improve articulation/space where an audition identifies a problem. A wholesale sample-library replacement is not justified by this review, especially after the owner's existing blind palette comparisons.

## Changes I would prioritize

1. **Let players reach the final resolution.** In *The Last Light*, the resolving D bell first enters at bar 26, about **79.1 seconds** into the music. The run-complete screen can be left before this point and stops its music on exit. There is no dwell-time evidence here proving how often it is missed, but placing the story's musical destination that late is a clear exposure risk. Keep the long version; create a concise game opening with one withheld phrase followed by the completed cadence within roughly 15–25 seconds, then a gentle sustain/loop. Alternatively, preserve the ending across the return-home transition. Do not make players wait before continuing. [Score](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/tools/music/scores/run_win.py#L45) · [screen exit](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/src/scenes/RunCompleteScene.js#L295).

2. **Expose Sera's answer in the Lieutenant theme.** Bars 41–42 place the small, unshadowed violin answer against strong ensemble leads and moving accompaniment. Withdraw the trumpet doubling and part of the string motor for those two bars; let the remaining ensemble hold rather than compete. Restore the mass afterwards. This is a particularly valuable place for clean bowing and phrase shape rather than pitch slides. The arrangement collision is visible in the score; actual masking needs an audition.

3. **Re-orchestrate calm versions that retain the full mix's busy motors.** The shared `Battle.spic8` and `spic16` helpers default to shared layers without calm attenuation. Some later themes therefore add the calm piano/solo/low-string bed while retaining busy spiccato writing. Start with *Iron Rain* and *Steel and Thread*: keep one pulse, remove alternate attacks or move the motor to full only, and leave a short space after the lead's question. Do this per score, not through an indiscriminate helper change. The original *Ember Dusk* already explicitly removes many motors in calm. Also audition *Iron Rain*’s calm C phrase an octave lower: its solo violin reaches **C7 at bar 34**, because the trumpet line was copied up an octave. That is a concrete register choice worth checking before EQ. [Shared arrangement defaults](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/tools/music/scores/_battle.py#L114).

4. **Give the most repeated themes genuine second statements.** Keep their recognizable opening cells; change the answer, harmonic destination, phrase length, or who interrupts them. For *Ember Dusk*, preserve the first presentation because the finale quotes it, but alter A2's last four bars before returning intact at A3. For *The Perfect Duel*, develop the pickup/held-note relationship rather than repeat the entire eight-bar melody three times. Timbre changes alone do not create the same degree of musical development.

5. **Make enrage replace an existing role when it introduces a new one.** The Dark Rider's crawl, Iron Wall's walking bass, and Blade Lord's perpetual-motion strings are good ideas. They currently add motion to arrangements that already contain moving basses or strings. Try replacing the competing original line. At equal playback level, the new behavior should be identifiable without knowing the filename.

6. **Clarify the score's reserved musical payoff.** The stale bible says the whole score is in D and rarely resolves there; the actual music spans many keys and contains ordinary local tonic arrivals. That is healthy. Reserve the **specific Hollow Sun ending and its final bell treatment**, rather than prohibit all tonic cadences. The finale already writes D5 bells at bars 29 and 37 despite the stated victory-only reservation. If the bell is intended to announce the final recovery of the name, change those earlier bell strikes to A/E, or explicitly adopt a different meaning for them. Preserve local resolutions that help the melodies breathe.

7. **Keep lossless masters and encode each delivery format directly.** The renderer creates a temporary 24-bit WAV, writes the web MP3, then deletes that WAV. iOS subsequently re-encodes the web MP3 at a lower quality. Keep a 24-bit or float lossless export and derive web and iOS assets independently. This removes avoidable generation loss; it does not establish that the current iOS files sound audibly poor.

8. **Use orchestration to preserve escalation across acts.** *The Border Holds* begins its main strain with a six-part march stack. Start narrower and reserve the biggest combination for the Thread/drill confrontation. Act II already uses choir in multiple field/boss cues despite the documented Act III arrival. Either reduce those earlier voices or define a deliberate distinction between early distant/human voices, later ritual choir, and the finale's people singing together. The payoff depends on a recognizable change in musical behavior, not simply the choir sample's presence.

## Direction from the world bible

Treat the bible as a dramatic guide, not a reason to rewrite good music to obey obsolete literal rules.

- **Border: people still have names.** Favor distinct solo colors, breathing phrases, folk or march gestures, and imperfect musical conversations. The joyful caravan and village fiddle are useful contrast; the whole game need not sound uniformly mournful.
- **Empire: drill replaces oath.** Keep accents and arrivals disciplined. Make rebellion audible through a flexible answer over a rigid pulse, rather than by making every imperial passage maximally thick.
- **Rite: human ceremony becoming wrong.** Preserve E/F friction, asymmetrical meter, unanswered phrases, and choral stillness. Reverb should support the sacred space while leaving the line intelligible.
- **Hearth and Deep: recognition fails.** Familiar material missing a reply is more specific than a new generic ominous theme. Keep the Act IV and Deep map transformations.
- **Finale: names return as people.** Let individual voices become a collective melody. Distinguish that choir from the earlier ritual pads with entrances, consonance, phrasing, and an actual tune. Keep the Entity itself without a conventional villain melody.

The bible's [music discussion](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/7d5c1af8f849139352a79715a5d1c30f84774e3b/docs/lore/01-cosmology.md#music-is-canon) and [imperial chant](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/7d5c1af8f849139352a79715a5d1c30f84774e3b/docs/lore/07-found-texts.md#the-imperial-drill-chant) support this direction. These are recommendations about musical storytelling, not a demand to preserve every historical claim in those documents.

### Act I

**Ember Dusk — [battle_act1.py](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/tools/music/scores/battle_act1.py).** Strong main-theme candidate: A–D–E–A has a clear reach, its offbeat held A is memorable, B (21–28) sequences the same reach down in a contrasting high line, and C (29–36) gives the Empire a different low-register identity. Preserve this anchor and its use in the finale. Improve A2 (13–20): the tune is literal A1 repeated; sixteenths, horns and stabs supply nearly all development. Let the last two bars vary rhythmically or let the horns answer the violin instead of duplicating the whole phrase. Save the top D6 for a clearer single peak rather than every repeat. Audition the glock in 21/23 as punctuation; no need for more high doubling. The end of MEL_A actually reaches D over Dm; do not enforce a ban on every tonic note just because the document reserves the final ritual cadence. Distinguish a local phrase landing from the unique full-ensemble/bell resolution at run victory.

**Border Marches — [battle_act1_2.py](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/tools/music/scores/battle_act1_2.py).** The 6/8 gallop and Oath interlude provide immediate contrast to Ember. The tune's landing on D before climbing is singable and the brass breath variants are thoughtful. **Open Ground — [battle_act1_3.py](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/tools/music/scores/battle_act1_3.py).** One of the strongest individual hooks: D→Bb on the push moves with the chord, then the long F creates room for the horn answer. **The Oath at the Ford — [battle_act1_4.py](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/tools/music/scores/battle_act1_4.py).** The best use of the Old Kingdom idea as a system: E pedal stays while the call's landings brighten B→C#→D; A′ replaces C# with C and a long violin descant makes the changed allegiance audible. ## Production, balance, and fidelity

**Measurement method:** FFmpeg EBU R128 loudness/LRA/true peak over each configured loop region; sample peaks over the entire decoded file; stereo measurements over loop regions. LRA measures variation in short-term loudness, not musical quality or a direct amount of compression. [Complete 88-file measurements](music-metrics.csv) accompany this report.


1. **Preserve lossless masters and make both delivery encodes from them.** Confirmed pipeline quality opportunity: [tools/music/engine/render.py:847–855](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/tools/music/engine/render.py#L847) temporarily writes 24-bit PCM, encodes web MP3 at LAME V4, and deletes the PCM. The optional WAV path at line 849 is 16-bit. [tools/ios/compactMusic.mjs:64–79](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/tools/ios/compactMusic.mjs#L64) decodes that already-lossy MP3 and re-encodes at V6. This compounds codec loss unnecessarily. Keep a PCM24 or float32 master per variant, encode web/iOS separately, and retain the existing duration checks. Preserve existing loop-point/gapless tests. Likely audition targets: violin starts, exposed harp/celesta, cymbal decay, choir and reverb tails; do not claim artifacts were heard. Upgrading the source performance/arrangement will have more musical impact than simply raising every delivery bitrate.

2. **Create breathing room in the most continuously intense loops before turning drums down again.** Measurements identify good audition priorities: “Against the Standard” (`music_battle_elite`) LRA 1.2 LU; “Old Kingdom Roads” (`music_battle_act2_3`) 1.4 LU; “Against the Rite” (`music_battle_act3_2`) 1.8 LU; Lieutenant full 1.7 LU; Act III enrage variants 1.6–1.7 LU; Emperor enrage 1.8 LU. LRA is short-term macro loudness variation, not a direct compression/quality score. These may be intentionally insistent. Try a 2–4-bar lower-density phrase, remove an ostinato under the main tune, delay brass reinforcement, or let a cadence breathe before the return. Retain rhythm where it carries identity. Do not fix low LRA by adding indiscriminate random expression or global volume swings. “Ember Dusk” 3.1 LU, “The Name Is Not Spoken” (act4_3) 5.7 LU, exploration Act IV 9.5 LU show the engine can already render larger contrast.

3. **Calm/full balance is consistent and should be preserved.** All 27 pairs measured: 22 are 3.0 LU apart, 3 are 3.5 LU apart, 1 is 3.2 LU apart, and 1 is 4.0 LU apart. Most full battle tracks sit at −14 LUFS and calm at −17 LUFS, most bosses at −13.5 LUFS. These are deliberate intensity differences, not faulty normalization. Compare revisions level-matched; otherwise the louder version will usually seem better. Keep calm/full common material on the same timeline. Current equal-gain crossfades in [src/utils/LoopedMusic.js:342–363](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/src/utils/LoopedMusic.js#L342) are sensible for correlated versions; do not swap blindly to equal-power curves, which can create a midpoint bump. Runtime rise is 1.2 seconds, settle 3 seconds, enrage 2.5 seconds in [BattleMusicController.js:31–33](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/src/ui/BattleMusicController.js#L31). Optional musical polish: align a new percussion entry to the next beat or bar, while urgent threat gain changes can remain immediate. This is an audition/design choice, not a correctness finding.

4. **There is a small summed-finale headroom edge case.** Every individual loop has a measured true peak ≤ −0.8 dBTP. Summing `music_boss_entity_finale` and `music_boss_entity_finale_hum` at gain 1 yields +0.1 dBTP / +0.049 dBFS sample peak; at hum gain 0.99 it still yields +0.1 dBTP / +0.029 dBFS sample peak, with one sample above 1 at ~46.772 s. At hum .9 sample peak is −0.161 dBFS. This is **low-priority mix polish**, not a claim of audible clipping in normal play: default music slider .5 applies .25 gain (−12.04 dB) via the quadratic curve ([AudioManager.js:65–67](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/src/utils/AudioManager.js#L65)), and actual HP usually continues falling. At full user volume and near-full boss HP, runtime sums the individually mastered files without another limiter ([LoopedMusic.js:113–120](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/src/utils/LoopedMusic.js#L113), `146–152`; [BattleMusicController.js:273–283](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/src/ui/BattleMusicController.js#L273)). Leave ~1–2 dB more joint headroom or measure all supported additive combinations; do not normalize the hum up to the same loudness as the orchestra. Its retreat with HP is a strong musical/gameplay idea.

5. **Keep the clean violin direction; realism should come from written phrasing and articulation choices.** The house palette already selects `sso_clean` ([palette.py:410–417](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/tools/music/engine/palette.py#L410)), and [perform.py:31–38](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/tools/music/engine/perform.py#L31) explicitly avoids unrequested marcato/bow-in/swell/bow dips, with a gentle phrase arch. Section strings use clean performance too. Respect that taste; do not recommend artificial portamento or stronger bow shaping globally. Highest-value next checks are appropriate note lengths, written breaths, deliberate slur boundaries, accents only where the melody needs them, and preserving the melodic foreground when sections join. The legacy sampler does have slide/swell options, but it would be misleading to infer that every shipped violin uses those settings. Per-score palette exceptions exist.

6. **Polish ordinary scene transitions without losing intentional dramatic cuts.** `AudioManager.playMusic` waits for the new track to load then stops old music at once ([AudioManager.js:159–160](https://github.com/virtu333/rogue-emblem/blob/00dde90133ed18cb2d56e0f6f8591f7ccff4befe/src/utils/AudioManager.js#L159)), followed by a fade-in of the new cue. This can chop a sustained note/reverb tail if a caller hasn't already arranged a fade. A short outgoing release or transition tail is worth auditioning for map→shop/rest/home flows; don't naively overlap unrelated harmonies for long periods. The Entity's hard cut, silence, hinge and scheduled downbeat are intentional and should remain a special dramatic treatment. This is a source-observed route, not an assertion that every scene change sounds bad.

### Production work order

First preserve source masters and compare representative cues at matched loudness. Next edit arrangement density and lead/answer relationships in the low-contrast battle loops. Then refine exposed instrument articulations, background reverb depth, and cue-to-cue transitions through targeted A/B listening. Finally verify all deliveries at low playback level, headphones, phone/mono speaker, and with game SFX; measure joint additive headroom and encoded loop boundaries. Avoid a blanket high-shelf boost, blanket percussion reduction, or a wholesale library change without comparative listening.

### Small-speaker audition priorities

A Hann-window FFT energy summary over loop regions (32,768-frame windows, both channels) gives: deep exploration 18.4% below 150 Hz + 69.1% at 150–500 Hz = **87.5% below 500 Hz**, only 12.5% at 500 Hz–4 kHz; Entity theme **69.6% below 500 Hz**, 30.2% at 500 Hz–4 kHz; Act IV exploration **51.2% below 500 Hz**, 48.8% at 500 Hz–4 kHz. Act I exploration comparison: **40.0% below 500 Hz**, 59.3% at 500 Hz–4 kHz. These are unweighted physical energy fractions, not perceived-loudness percentages, so do not declare any cue inaudible. Combined with deep exploration's −24 LUFS, this makes deep exploration the first mobile-speaker audition target: try an extremely restrained midrange octave/harmonic answer carrying the essential pitch gesture if the intended tension disappears on phone playback, preserving the sparse low drone. Entity already has substantially more midrange; don't brighten everything globally. Data in `spectral-metrics.json`.
