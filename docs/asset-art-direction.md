# Art direction: identity before polish

## User direction

Use the original sprites as the starting point. Keep core character and class characteristics, strengthen silhouettes, and develop distinctive themes grounded in this game's world. Avoid generic fantasy and modern mobile-game styling. Edric retains his approved teal green-blue identity. The approved silhouette approach is a readability principle, not a uniform costume for every unit.

## Reference order for each generation

1. Original sprite and relevant named-character/class variant: preserve signature hair, clothing, weapon, posture and recognizable identity.
2. Actual character/class lore and mechanical role from repository data.
3. Approved weathered terrain and silhouette studies for pixel density, palette and contrast.
4. One specific proposed visual interpretation, clearly distinguished from established lore.

Do not replace distinct original designs with copies of the new teal knight. Teal is Edric's signature; do not automatically give every ally the same teal tabard. Faction readability also uses markers, outlines and silhouette, not costume color alone.

## Proposed interpretations grounded in existing class lore

These are art proposals, not new canonical backstories.

- Knight: the empire's human wall. Broad slab shield, rectangular armor masses, a worn issue tabard and planted stance. Preserve the original armor/weapon identity. Avoid decorative giant pauldrons. Current generated knight remains a readability study pending this identity pass.
- Fighter: a conscript from logging camps or quarries. Workwear silhouette, reinforced gloves and a practical axe; strength expressed through posture rather than exaggerated muscles.
- Cavalier: the old kingdom's cavalry under new command. Disciplined lance posture, practical tack and a faded heraldic cloth with a visibly overstitched replacement emblem. Readable horse/rider separation comes first.
- Archer: a border yeoman. Weather cloak, simple bow and distinctive quiver mass; no matching red superhero suit required to communicate an enemy.
- Mage: an academy-trained field caster. Structured school clothing adapted for travel, worn book and one recognizable cuff/collar shape. Avoid obligatory enormous wizard hats or constant magical glow.
- Cleric: temple attendants serving with the levies. Travel-worn vestments, a plain staff and a small field satchel. Preserve sacred identity without immaculate white-and-gold ornament everywhere.
- Thief: a practical wartime scavenger. Short asymmetrical coat, agile posture and visible tools; avoid a default all-black assassin costume.
- Wyvern Rider: a mountain-clan rider. Rugged harness, weatherproof layered clothing and practical bindings. Let mount posture and rider gear distinguish the class from imperial cavalry.
- Pegasus Knight: an independent sky-legion rider. Light equipment and a clear upright lance silhouette; preserve mount elegance without jeweled armor or decorative feather overload.
- Mercenary: maintained, mixed-origin equipment and an economical stance. A clear sword silhouette and repaired gear convey experience without excessive belts and trinkets.

## Heroes, terrain and nodes

Named heroes need individual briefs after inspecting their originals and character text. Do not infer personalities from names or class alone. Edric's teal and Sera's existing hair/robe identity remain anchors.

Terrain and campaign nodes should share regional construction, worn materials, banners and signs of use. A shop can feel like a traveling supplier; a fort like a functioning garrison; a church like shelter in a wartime world. These are proposed visual treatments, not changes to node mechanics. Distinguish biomes and cultures through shape/material choices, not merely palette swaps. Avoid ubiquitous skulls, spikes, glowing runes or grimdark decoration.

## Small-scale discipline

Give each class one dominant silhouette idea and one secondary identifying motif. At gameplay scale, detail that becomes noise should be removed. Wear should appear as deliberate large marks, not texture speckling. Keep weapon, pose and face/hair identity readable. Promotions should develop the base class's visual story and authority, not just enlarge armor.

## Review rubric

Can we recognize the original? Can we identify the class without its label? Does it express this world's lore? Is it distinct from adjacent classes? Does it work over terrain at phone zoom? If polished art loses any of these, revise before expanding the family.
