# 1 · Cosmology

What the world is made of, how it works, and why it is dying. Everything else
in this bible leans on this file.

---

## The Stillness

Before the first morning there was no *after*. Nothing followed anything. The
liturgy's first page puts it in five words: **"Before her, nothing came next."**

That condition was not empty. It had weight and extent. It was, and it still
is, **the Stillness**: the world as it is when nothing happens to it. It lies
beneath the land the way bedrock lies beneath soil. It is not a creature,
though it behaves like one when disturbed, and it is not a god, though Edric
calls it one.

It is known by several names, none of which it has:

| Who | Word | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The liturgy | *nothing*, a blank where a noun would go | To name it would be to give it a thread, an end, an *after*. The seers were never sure whether that would kill it or wake it. |
| Common folk, deeds | **the Sleeper** | Because it sleeps, and because saying so is a prayer. |
| The court circle | **the Entity** | A clerk's word: neutral, tidy, countable. The game's UI inherited it. |
| Seers, privately | **the Before** | Where everything goes when it stops. |
| Where it is | **the Deep** | The place, not the thing. |

**What it does.** Nothing, on purpose. It does not hate, want or scheme. When
it stirs, the world around it loses its *after*: water stops flowing without
freezing, the dead stop staying down, prayers stop being answered, futures
stop branching. That loss, felt from the outside, is what the game calls **the
corruption**. When the Sleeper turns over, the land warps. The war is
also the sound of it turning over. Each feeds the other: war thins names, thin
names let the Sleeper stir, and its stirring makes people desperate enough to
go to war.

**What it keeps.** Everything that falls into it stays there, including every
name fed to it and every future unmade. Its body in the Deep is "a texture of
eyes" (the portrait pass's phrase), and that is literal **(II)**: it sees with
what it has swallowed. That is why "IT REMEMBERS YOU" is carved on the last
threshold, and why the Sleeper, which has no voice, has begun to **hum**.

## The Dawn

Something answered the Stillness, and the answer was the first morning.

The faith calls her **the Dawn**. Seers call her **the Weaver**. Old wives say
**Our Lady of the Answer**, and the empire's clerks, when they must, say
**the Hollow**. Her name is the one thing this bible does not write down. It
cannot be written. See [the Root](#music-is-canon).

She did three things, and the liturgy's First Pages are about them:

1. **She made *after*.** She laid time over the Stillness the way a weaver lays
   weft across warp, one thread after another, so that one thing could follow
   another. The world is her cloth. Seers call it **the weave**. The route map
   is a loom because the seers could never picture the future any other way.
2. **She named things.** Naming is how the cloth holds: a thing with a name
   has a shape it can return to. The first things she named were the
   **dragons**, then fire, wind, thunder and the other elements, then rivers,
   mountains and, last, people. The words she used are **the Old Tongue**.
   Anima magic is saying those words aloud ([Magic](#magic)). Dragon breath is
   the same words, spoken by the first things that ever heard them. "Fire
   Breath: the oldest word in the dragon tongue."
3. **She answered.** Every prayer since has been an attempt to call back to
   her. The devout temperament's line is correct doctrine: *"Not every prayer
   is answered. Every prayer is heard."*

## The Starfall and the Spending

The Sleeper turned over for the first time. Why, nobody knows ([Mysteries](#deliberate-mysteries)).
The cloth tore. Mountains opened, a year of storms walked the land, and the
new-named things began to forget themselves.

The Dawn could not fight the Stillness. It was the ground her cloth lay on;
you cannot fight the floor. She did the only thing a name can do against
something that has none. **She spent it.**

She broke her name into more pieces than there are stars and let them fall.
Folk memory calls that night **the Starfall**, "the night the stars came
down", and the bow called **Starfall** was strung by moonlight while it
happened. The largest piece struck the lowland where the fens are now. It
left a crater that filled with black, perfectly still water: **the Glass**.
The dragons, her first-named, lay down in a ring around it to hold the
breach closed with their bodies and their dreams. Over ages they became the
standing stones of **the Hallow**, the sacred ground.

The next morning the sun rose **hollow**: a black disc with a thin gold ring.
It has risen that way ever since.

What the Dawn bought with her name is the central fact of the world:

> **Every name anyone has ever carried is a splinter of hers.**

The pieces did not stay in the sky. They settled on everything and everyone
that would ever be named. A child named at a roadside shrine receives a
splinter. A river that people keep calling by its name keeps its splinter.
While the world is full of names that are held, spoken and remembered, it is
full of *after*, and the Stillness sleeps beneath it. The Dawn is not gone.
She is asleep in every name.

That makes **two sleepers**: the one below, the Stillness, and the one within,
the Dawn, scattered through everyone. The whole history of the world is a
balance between them. When names thin, the Sleeper below stirs. When names
are gathered and spoken together, the one within begins to wake. Edric's line
to the Emperor, *"Power stolen from a sleeping god. We're here to return
it,"* is truer than he knows. The power the Emperor stole was hers, one name
at a time.

## Names

The rules of names are the physics of this world. They explain why the
shipped text reads the way it does, and they should decide new text.

### What a name is

A name is a splinter of the Dawn, held by a person or a thing. It gives its
holder a shape to return to and a thread in the weave, a future. It shines.
The Hollow Sun's corona is made of every name still held, seen from far off.

### How names are kept

A name is kept by being **held, spoken and remembered**. The world is full of
institutions that exist, whether they know it or not, to keep names:

| Keeper | Practice | In the shipped text |
|---|---|---|
| **Shrines** | A child is named at a shrine for a copper; the shrine writes the name down and keeps it. | "The roadside shrine asks only a copper and a name. It keeps both." |
| **The temple** | The *book of returns*. Revival works by reading a person's name back to them until the body remembers its shape. | "Breathe. Good. Now remember who you are." / "The book is nearly full." |
| **Border banner-houses** | The lord learns every name behind him. | Lord class lore; Edric counting. |
| **Border wardens** | Two ledgers, routes and the dead. The ledger of the dead holds the names so the dark cannot take them. | Ranger lore; Voss's ledger. |
| **Smith-saints** | Names struck into iron do not fade. | Varen's mark, two centuries running. |
| **Bards** | Songs carry names past checkpoints that cannot tax a song. | Bard lore and lines. |
| **Soldiers** | An epithet given by soldiers is a name the Roll cannot take. | Hero: "given by soldiers, never by command, and never taken back." |

**Epithets are armor.** A name with deeds woven onto it (*Who Held the
Bridge*, *the Untouched*, *Lantern of the March*) is heavier and harder to
spend. That is why the game's deeds system is not decoration: an epithet
makes a unit's name brighter, and the ceremony band is right to burn it on.

**Oaths need names.** An oath is sworn *by* a name: the king's, the
kingdom's, the Dawn's. If the name is spent, the oath breaks. This is what
happened on the Unsworn Night, and it is why the empire runs on drill:
**"The empire kept the drills and dropped the oaths."** It had to. There
was nothing left to swear by.

### How names are lost

- **Forgetting.** Slow and natural. A village no one visits loses its name in
  three generations.
- **Spending.** A holder gives a name up deliberately to buy something. The
  Dawn spent hers to buy sleep. The old kings spent theirs at the Hearthstone.
  The berserk school spends its pupils' names to buy fearlessness ("the red").
  Sera is spending hers to buy threads **(III)**.
- **Feeding.** Someone *else* spends a name. It is taken, written into the
  Roll in red ink and given to the Sleeper at the Hearthstone. This is the
  empire's whole art, and the thing the old kingdom's Oath forbade.
- **Unmaking.** When the Sleeper stirs near something, the thing's name
  thins. Shrine boards go blank ("The paint didn't fade. The words did."),
  mile-marker numbers wear away, the seers' notes stop mid-sentence.

### The gradient

Names are thickest where the Sleeper is farthest and thinnest over its body.
This is a rule for writers:

| Region | Names |
|---|---|
| **Border Marches** (Act I) | Still named. Rivers, fords, halls, smiths and people all have names. Hagen paints his on his cart. |
| **Old Kingdom Roads** (Act II) | The empire has renumbered the provinces. Towns go by old names in private and by numbers on signs. |
| **The fens, the Hallow** (Act III) | Words fade off boards. Villages are "the drowned village". People are named, but softly. |
| **The Hearth, the Seat** (Act IV) | Nothing is named. The capital's heraldry office is ash. Streets are "swept clean". |
| **The Deep** | Nothing has ever been named. |

The route of every run is a walk down that gradient. The world gets darker
because it gets more nameless, and it gets more nameless because it is closer
to the thing that eats names.

### Names that came back

A name fed to the Sleeper is not destroyed. It is swallowed and kept. When the
Sleeper is wounded, what it swallowed comes out. See
[the finale](#the-finale-is-a-return).

## The Hollow Sun and the Eclipse

**The Hollow Sun** is the sun as it has been since the Starfall: a black disc
where the Dawn's name used to shine, ringed by a thin gold corona. The corona
is **the light of every name still held**. Theologians argued about this for
centuries. The empire settled it by forbidding the argument.

When names thin, the corona thins. The Grey Winter dimmed it; the Unsworn
Night took a visible bite; thirty-four years of feeding have left the land in
a permanent dusk. Only Act IV and the Deep have true night.

**The Eclipse** (the run clock) is an ink disc crossing the Hollow Sun. It is
the **court circle's feeding, seen from below (II)**. While the warband is in
the field, the cantors at the Hearthstone go on reading names from the Roll
into the dark. Every turn a battle runs is more names read. The disc is the
Roll's ink.

| Game fact | Canon |
|---|---|
| Shadow is committed only at victory | A battle's outcome is not woven into the thread until it is won. Until then, Sera's sight holds it open. |
| Phases **Pale → Waning → Umbral → Totality → Hollow** | The seers' old terms for the corona's thinning, from the Dawn-office's calendar of vigils. |
| Nodes fall from the outer lanes inward | The feeding reaches the edges of Sera's sight first, where she is weakest. |
| A fallen node becomes *Burned village, Desecrated chapel, Lost to the dark, Silent arena* | Places whose names were read into the dark. A "would-be ally" lost to the dark is a person whose name was fed before you reached them. |
| **Sun flares** −3 when an act boss falls | An officer's name, held by the Roll, flares back into the corona when he dies. |
| **Kindle** at a chapel | Relighting the chapel's name-lamp and reading its book of names aloud. Kindling is a genuine liturgy: *the Vigil of Lamps*. |
| The eclipse bell calls; no answer comes | A bell calling a name that no longer answers. |

The score keeps the Eclipse musically separate from the Entity, and canon
agrees. The Eclipse is **human work**, the empire's feeding. It has a bell
because people rang it. The Sleeper has no bell; it only hums.

## The Hallow and the Hearth

The Sleeper is vast. The whole old kingdom lies on it, and two places sit
nearest the surface. Both were holy before the empire; both are held by the
empire now.

**The Hallow: its breath.** In the lowland fens, the ring of standing stones
around the Glass. The Glass is the crater where the Dawn's largest splinter
fell, a black mere so still it is a mirror. Where the Sleeper *breathes*, the
fens rise and fall with it: "something big breathes under the fen. The water
rises when it does." The stones are the dragons who lay down to hold the
breach, grown over with ages. The seer order kept the Hallow.

**The Hearth: its heart.** A mountain to the east whose stone is warm all
year. The old kings found a hall in its roots, the **Hearthstone**, where the
stone beats. "Below the capital the stone is warm, and the warmth has a
pulse." The kings kept the Hearth; each went down once, at his crowning.
The empire built **the Seat** on the mountain's shoulder, directly above it.

**Between them runs the Sleeper's body**, under the fens, the grain
provinces and the uplands. The **far side of the Glass** is the Hearthstone.
Anyone who goes down through the Glass and survives comes up in the Deep,
beneath the Seat. This is how Normal's thread reaches the Lieutenant without
crossing the capital ([08 § Routes](08-reconciliation.md#the-three-routes)),
and it is where the Lieutenant's epithet comes from.

From high enough, the rivers, fens and uplands of the realm trace the outline
of something curled up asleep. The sky legions knew this. It was on their
charts, and it is one reason the empire disbanded them **(II)**.

## The Thread, the weave and the loop

### The Thread

When the Dawn spent her name, one thing of hers was not spent: her **sight**
along the weave. It fell with the largest splinter into the Glass. The first
seers found it there. They called it **the Thread**. It is held by one seer at
a time and passes on when the holder dies. The seer order existed to keep it.

A seer holding the Thread sees possible futures as threads of weft running
across the fixed warp of the world. They can walk forward along one, see where
it frays and choose another. Sera holds it now. She is the last.

### Why runs repeat

A run is **one thread of the March of the year 34 of the Seat**. Sera walks
it with the warband. If Edric falls, the thread is cut. A cut thread does not
simply end. It falls into the Stillness, which keeps everything, and Sera
begins again from the one fixed point her sight can always return to: **the
night before the March**, at the fire by the Ford. That fire is home base. It
is the last ember of the old kingdom's Hearth, carried west by the Last
King's son ([Chronicle](02-chronicle.md)).

| Game system | What it is inside the world |
|---|---|
| Home base between runs | The fire on the night before. Sera sits by it and chooses the next thread. |
| Meta-progression | What Sera remembers from cut threads: allies she has met, roads she has learned, how to see more clearly. |
| Blessings | Pieces of liturgy and prayer Sera takes with her into a thread. |
| Vision / Rewind | Sera glancing along the thread and pulling it back a stitch. Finite, because every pull costs her. |
| The Loom (route map) | Her sight of this thread's roads. Lanes are warp; the path is the weft she has woven. |
| Déjà vu, boss memories | The Sleeper's memories of cut threads, seeping upward. See below. |
| Difficulty modes | Different threads of the same year, each drawn tighter. See [08 § Routes](08-reconciliation.md#the-three-routes). |

### Who remembers

Memory of other threads leaks out of the Sleeper, and it leaks most into
those nearest it:

- **Ordinary soldiers** feel déjà vu, rarely. ("Why do I always remember the crow?")
- **Border bosses** (Act I) dream it. ("From a dream where I died.")
- **Imperial officers** (Act II) have nightmares they report as faith, or, in
  the Archmage's case, as data. ("So the rifts do loop. I kept notes.")
- **The Lieutenant's champions** (Act III) have been *told* by him, and half
  believe it.
- **The Lieutenant** sees the cut threads from underneath, all of them.
- **Hagen** drowned in the Glass twice and came back both times. He remembers
  everything and has decided that is a business advantage.
- **The Sleeper** remembers every thread it has swallowed. "It remembers...
  unmaking us."

The enemy calls a cut thread a **rift**. That word is the court circle's,
chosen by the Archmage. Seers never use it.

### Why winning does not end it

The runStart lines after a victory ("Even victory frays") are true. One
thread that holds does not hold the Sleeper. **A cloth holds.** Every victory
Sera weaves is a thread laid into a cloth strong enough, one day, to lay back
over the Stillness for good. That gives repeated victories meaning: each one
thickens the weave. It is what her Normal-victory line means by *"It's my
turn to guard the dream."*

## Magic

All magic in the world is one of three things done to names.

| School | What it does to names | Where learned | In game |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anima** (fire, thunder, wind) | **Says** an Old Tongue name. Speak fire's first name and fire remembers it is fire, right here. | The academy (burned), private tutors, primers | Mages, Sages, tomes. "Read the words aloud and the fire follows." Excalibur is "wind, written down." |
| **Light** | **Calls** a name back to its holder. Healing reminds a body of its shape; holy light reminds a thing of what it is, which hurts things that have forgotten. | The temple, the seer order | Staves, Light tomes, Clerics, Bishops, Sera. Revival is reading the book of returns. |
| **Dark** (the Unlit) | **Unsays** a name. Takes a piece of someone's name and keeps it. | The court circle, from the Middle Pages | Dark Knights, Warlocks, Nosferatu. "Whatever a warlock's spell tears out of a foe flows back into the caster." |

Around those three:

- **The Thread.** Not a school. It is one thing, and one person holds it.
- **Blood-price.** Blood carries a name the way ink carries a word. Weapon arts
  cost HP because they spend a little of the wielder's name for a moment of
  more. The Scroll Archive's seers "inked every art in the reader's blood" for
  the same reason. The smith-saints' **Blood Forge** quenches an edge in the
  smith's own name. They cursed it and it works.
- **Dragon breath.** The Old Tongue in the mouths that heard it first. No
  mortal throat can say it, only survive it.
- **Blessings and oaths.** Contracts sworn by names. They work as long as the
  names they are sworn by are held.

**Why "the light" feels impersonal.** Holy knights say the blessing is a
formality and the mending works anyway. They are right: light works because a
splinter of the Dawn is in the healer and the healed alike. It needs no priest.
It needs two names.

## The corruption

The corruption is **the Sleeper's nearness, visible**. Close to it, things
lose their *after*:

- Water forgets to flow and goes black and still ("water's black past the reeds").
- Bog forgets to swallow ("walked through the bog and didn't sink").
- Colour drains, because colour is how a name looks from outside. Act III is
  *Bleached*.
- The dead forget to stay down. A body whose name was **fed** has nothing to
  call it home. A body whose name is still **written in the Roll** keeps
  obeying the Roll. Those are revenants: dead levy men still on the muster.
  Fen-village rule: "burn the dead, or give them to the light."
- The woken dragons forget which order they lay down in. "The ritual is
  waking them in the wrong order."
- Light goes wrong when it has nothing left to call on: Bleached Rite's
  "light too bright to be kind" is holiness with no names left in it.

The **Berserker King** is untouched by it because there is nothing left in him
to drain. He spent his own name, in rage, decades ago.

## Colour is canon

The Art Bible's palette is diegetic. It is how names look.

| Colour | Canon |
|---|---|
| **Ember gold** — you | Names held and spoken. Your units carry light at night because they have names. The Hollow Sun's corona is the same gold. |
| **Blood crimson** — the empire | The Roll's red ink. Everything written into the Roll, from the levy to its officers, wears it. "Enemies carry no light of their own": their names are on paper in the Seat, not in them. |
| **Verdigris** — allies | Copper left out in weather. The colour of names kept by others: villages, shrines, strangers you rescue. "A copper and a name." |
| **Unlight violet** — corruption | What the Sleeper has swallowed. Colour draining toward it. |
| **Steel blue** — the player's rank and file | Names under arms: held, but tested. |
| **Teal** — Edric | The old kingdom's colour, before the crimson. The Border Crown kept it. Edric is the only one who wears it, because nobody else is heir to it. |
| **Cream** — Sera | The seer order's undyed habit. Seers wear no colour so as not to be mistaken for a name. |

## Music is canon

The score is built around one withheld note. In canon, that note is her name.

| Motif | Canon |
|---|---|
| **The Root (D)** | The Dawn's name, as near as sound can get. The whole score is in D and almost never resolves to it. |
| **The Thread** (A–D–E–A) | Sera's sight reaching for the Root, touching it in passing, never resting there. |
| **The Hollow Sun** (climbs to C#, stops) | The world trying to say her name and stopping one note short. The church choir falls silent where the name would go because nobody can sing it. |
| **The Old Kingdom** (open-fourths horn) | The horn blown at the Ford when the first king swore the Oath. It calls names to gather. |
| **The Empire** (D–E♭–D–C, falling to B♭) | The Sleeper's hum set to marching. The Marshal heard the hum at the Hearthstone on the Unsworn Night, the Root with its semitone shadow, and the drill-masters built the imperial cadence on it **(II)**. Marching in step to it empties a soldier a little, day by day. It is a slow feeding. |
| **The Lieutenant** (the Thread falling, a tritone behind) | A thread seen from underneath, where every line runs backward into its ending. |
| **Unlight** (hum on D and its shadow) | The Sleeper humming. It has swallowed so many splinters of the Dawn that it has begun to hum her note, with its own shadow beneath. It is trying to say the name it ate. |
| **The Eclipse bell** | A human bell calling a name that does not answer. |
| **Promotion resolves to D** | A promotion is a rite that adds to a name. For a moment the name is heavy enough to touch the Root. |
| **The Last Light** (bells toll D) | The name said. |

### The finale is a return

When the Sleeper is first wounded, the hum stops for the first time in the
score. There is silence, then one violin plays the Thread with its answer.
Then **All of Us Across**, and the choir that Act I never had sings Ember Dusk.

**The choir is the names coming back.** Wounding the Sleeper opens it, and
everything it swallowed pours out: the levy, the conquered, the burned
villages, the Lieutenant's name, Voss's wife, the old kingdom's own name,
thirty-four years of the Roll. They sing the song of the road you walked,
because they are free and that is the first song they hear. The Sleeper's hum
fades with its HP because the names are leaving it. The complete Thread
arrives only when everyone sings it: the Dawn's name can only be said by all
of her splinters at once. The phrase stops on the leading tone and is finished
by the Last Light, starting with the killing blow. For one chord the world
says her name, and the Sleeper, emptied of what it stole and lulled by the
one sound it was made to answer, sinks back into true sleep.

That is the true ending, and it is why the game is called what it is. The
Dawn does not come back. A dawn is made anyway, by everyone, without her. The
empire would call that a rogue dawn.

## Hidden canon (tier III)

Facts that are true, that no one in the world knows whole, and that game
text should never state. They exist so every hint the team writes points the
same way.

1. **Every name is a splinter of the Dawn's.** The faith teaches that she
   *spent* her name; it does not teach where it went. Learned seers suspected.
   The Last Pages say so, in the part nobody reads aloud.
2. **The Sleeper hums her note** because it has eaten so much of her.
3. **The finale choir is the freed names.**
4. **Sera is spending her own name to buy threads.** She was named
   **Serafen** at the chapter-house. Each thread she re-weaves costs a little
   more; "Sera" is what is left. Hagen, who has seen every thread, is the only
   one who ever calls her by the whole of it. If the March succeeds she keeps
   what remains. The Lunatic ending, where she can no longer see what comes
   next, means she has spent her *sight*, not her name.
5. **The Lieutenant was a boy called Tamlin**, a novice of the same
   chapter-house, taken at seven and drowned in the Glass. His name is still
   in the order's novice roll, which Sera carries. The fen shrine bells that
   ring "twice, then silence, then twice" are ringing it.
6. **The Emperor's name is gone.** He fed it first, as the price of the first
   bargain. No record, no person and not this bible hold it.
7. **The old kingdom's name is inside the Sleeper** and comes out with the
   choir. It is never written in any text. If the game ever shows it, it
   should be a gap: a line, a held breath, a bell.
8. **Hagen has walked more threads than Sera.** He is always ahead because
   he started earlier ([05](05-dramatis-personae.md#hagen)).
9. **The Last King's crown lies in the Wend at the Ford.** The river runs slow
   there. Nobody has looked, because the Iron Captain's order closed the
   crossing.
10. **The Dark Rider is a mantle.** The current rider is the fourth. The first
    was the courier who carried the Last King's order to fall back and sold it.

## Deliberate mysteries

Questions the world never answers. Writers should circle them, never close them.

- **Why did the Sleeper first turn over?** Seers argue: the Dawn's weaving
  woke it; something older disturbed it; or it dreams, and dreams move.
- **What would naming the Sleeper do?** The Light tome **Luce** was "written
  to end one thing under the sacred ground". Its author tried to give the
  Stillness a name, and with it an ending. "The rest is ash." Would a named
  Sleeper die, or wake with a future? This is the question behind any
  post-game ("the Sleeper stirs again").
- **Does the Dawn know?** Sera thinks "the goddess still remembers us". Some
  prayers do seem heard.
- **Is Hagen still human?**
- **Who carved WE CAME THIS FAR ALSO?** The chronicle says the first king's
  companions. The stone is older than that.
- **What do the dragons dream?** The mountain clans say wyverns hatch from it.
