# Rogue Dawn — World Bible

> *The goddess's name was not forgotten; it was spent.* — Goddess Icon

This folder is the history and canon of the world Rogue Dawn is set in. It
starts where the game's text, art and music already agree and fills in what
lies underneath them: where the world came from, what the empire is for, why
nothing here has a name, who every boss was before they had a title, and why
the finale's choir is singing.

It was written after a full inventory of every line of lore, dialogue,
art-direction note and score comment in the repository (September 2026). All
of that is kept. Where the existing text disagreed with itself, the
disagreement is resolved in [08-reconciliation.md](08-reconciliation.md), with
the data edits it recommends.

## The world on one page

Before anything happened there was the **Stillness**: no after, no change, no
names. It is still there, under the land. The liturgy calls it nothing at all,
common people call it **the Sleeper**, and the court circle's clerks, who like
tidy words, call it **the Entity**.

Something answered the Stillness, and the answer was morning. The faith calls
her **the Dawn**. She wove time over the Stillness like cloth over a sleeper
and gave the first things their names. When the Sleeper turned over in its
sleep the first time, she could not fight it, because it was the ground under
everything. So she **spent her name**, paid it out into the world the way
you spend a coin, on the night folk remember as the Starfall. Every name
anyone has carried since is part of her change. A world full of spoken names
is a world with an *after* in it, and while it stays that way the Sleeper
stays asleep. The sun has been hollow since that night, a black disc with a
thin gold corona (border folk call it *the rim*). The corona is made of every
name still spoken.

The **old kingdom** was founded on that knowledge. Its first king swore the
Oath at the Ford: *the strong serve the weak, for every name holds the
dark down equally*. Its kings gave up their own names at the Hearthstone to
sing the Sleeper deeper. For six hundred years that was enough.

Then it was not. Plague and famine thinned the names, and the Sleeper stirred.
The kingdom's Lord Marshal found in the stolen middle pages of the liturgy
that the Sleeper could be **fed**: any name, anyone's, and it would give
power back. On the **Unsworn Night** he spent the kingdom's own name. Every
oath sworn by it broke at once, and what held his soldiers together after was
drill, not oath. He called what he built **the Empire**, a word and not a
name, and he has fed it names for thirty-four years: the levy's, the
conquered's, his officers', his own.

He believes he is holding the dark back. He is fattening it. The empire's
dream and the Sleeper's nature turn out to be the same thing, a world where
nothing comes after.

**Sera**, the last seer, holds the Dawn's one unspent thing, **the Thread**:
sight along the weave of what could happen. **Edric**, last heir of the crown
that went into the river at the Ford, counts every name behind his banner. The
imperial curfew forbids light after dusk; clerks call any lamp lit against it
a *rogue light*. An imperial broadsheet posted in the Marches called the
warband that marched east from the border a **rogue dawn**. The warband kept the name.

Every run is a thread Sera weaves through one year of that March. When a
thread is cut it falls into the Sleeper, which keeps it, and that is why the
dead half-remember. When a thread holds all the way down, the names the
Sleeper ate come back out of it singing, and for the length of one chord the
world says the Dawn's name again. Nobody can say it alone.

## Tiers of truth

Canon here is written at three depths. Game text should draw from them in
that order, and rarely from the third.

| Tier | Who knows it | How it may appear in game |
|---|---|---|
| **I. Common** | Soldiers, merchants, villagers | Anywhere. Rumor, complaint, graffiti, shop tags |
| **II. Learned** | Seers, the court circle, old wardens, chroniclers | Relic lore, boss lines, liturgy fragments, rare flavor |
| **III. Hidden** | Nobody alive knows all of it | *Sealed* facts: never stated, only pointed at. *Earned* facts: stated once, in a specific moment the player has earned, never in a common pool |

Paragraphs are marked **(II)** or **(III)** where the tier matters; unmarked
text is common knowledge. Tier III facts are listed together in
[01-cosmology.md § Hidden canon](01-cosmology.md#hidden-canon-tier-iii), each
marked sealed or earned, so the team can see them in one place.

## Rules that follow from the canon

1. **Names are scarce and heavy.** The unnamed world the shipped text already
   builds, with its "the old kingdom", "the empire", "the lieutenant" and
   bosses who are only titles, is not a gap to fill. It is the world. See
   [01 § Names](01-cosmology.md#names). Proper names grow commoner the farther
   a place or person is from the ritual: the border still names its rivers;
   the Seat names nothing.
2. **Imperial officers have no names.** They surrender them to the Roll on
   commission and answer to rank. Every boss is a title for this reason.
   Their names can come back when they fall ([09-channeling.md](09-channeling.md)).
3. **The Sleeper is not evil and not a god.** It is the Stillness. It has no
   voice, wants nothing, and remembers everything it has eaten.
4. **The Dawn is not coming back as a person.** She is in everybody. The ending
   is people, together, not a goddess returning.
5. **The loop is real, faint and costly.** Memory of other threads grows
   stronger the nearer someone stands to the Sleeper. Soldiers get déjà vu;
   the Archmage keeps notes; Hagen has given up pretending.
6. **Keep the existing voice rules.** [lore-style-guide.md](../lore-style-guide.md)
   still governs how any of this is *written* in game. This folder decides
   *what is true*.
