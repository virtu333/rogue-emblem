# 4 · Powers, Orders and Institutions

Everyone who holds power in this world does one thing to names: **keeps**
them, **spends** them or **feeds** them. The fastest way to write a faction
correctly is to know which.

| Power | Names | Colour | Motto or saying |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Empire | Feeds | Crimson and iron | *What is, remains.* |
| The court circle | Feeds, and profits | Crimson and black | *Every name is a sum.* |
| The old kingdom / Border Crown | Keeps | Teal and gold | *The strong serve the weak.* |
| The temple (chaplains) | Keeps | Undyed and candle-gold | *Once more, and then once more after that.* |
| The temple (cathedral) | Surrendered | Crimson over white | *Render to the Seat.* |
| The seer order | Keeps the Thread | Cream | *Hold on to the thread.* |
| Border wardens | Keep (the ledger) | Moss green | *Still standing.* |
| Smith-saints | Keep (in iron) | Soot | *A mark for every maker.* |
| The berserk school | Spends its own | Red | *Forget, and fear goes with it.* |
| Dueling halls | Tried names; now trade | White gloves | *A duelist is a question with a sharp answer.* |
| The sky legions | Kept the map | Sky and white | *The mount picks the rider.* |
| Peak clans | Keep the oldest | None; no banners | *We were here first.* |
| Hill clans | Keep in blood-song | Ochre and red | *Sing them or lose them.* |
| Mercenary guild | Keeps contracts | Whatever pays | *My reputation is my wages.* |
| Bards and troupes | Smuggle | Motley | *Checkpoints cannot tax a song.* |

## The Empire

**What it is.** The old kingdom's own people and land, plus three conquered
neighbors, ruled from the Seat on the Hearth by a man with no name, through a
register of everyone else's.

**What it wants.** The Emperor's goal, never stated to anyone, is **a world in
which nothing comes next**: no rebellion, no succession, no decline, no
future, no end. He first went down to the Hearthstone to save the kingdom from
the Sleeper. Over thirty-four years he has come to want what the Sleeper is.
The imperial motto, carved over the Seat's gate, is ***What is, remains.***
The seers read it as a prayer to the Stillness, and it is.

**How it works.**

- **The Roll.** Every subject is registered in red ink, and names are held
  "in trust for the Seat". The Roll is kept in copies: provincial rolls,
  levy rolls, the officers' roll, and the Great Roll in the Seat, which the
  circle reads at the Hearthstone.
- **Rank instead of name.** Officers surrender their names on commission and
  are thereafter only their rank: Captain, Commander, General. Titles become
  names (*Iron Captain*, *Iron Wall*). Soldiers nickname officers, and the
  nicknames stick, because the soldiers are the only people still naming
  anything.
- **Drill instead of oath.** The imperial cadence is built on the Sleeper's
  hum. Soldiers who march to it for years become quiet, obedient and
  lightless. "Imperial elites. Not conscripts — true believers." The true
  believers are the ones the drill has emptied furthest.
- **The levy.** Each numbered province owes bodies. The levy takes bodies for
  the army and names for the Roll, and takes them AGAIN. Levy scrip is
  worthless west of the Roads.
- **The curfew.** No light after dusk without a warrant. Unwarranted light is a
  *rogue light*; a lamp in a window is a crime. As the corona dims, the
  curfew moves earlier.
- **The March.** The border administration, commanded by the Warden of the
  March (the Iron Captain) and signed in iron.
- **The Heartland.** The fens, closed crown demesne since S 25.

**What its people are like.** Tired. The empire is not a hive of villains;
it is a country that got used to something. Garrisons go unpaid; toll-keepers
depart; quartermasters write *Morale, don't ask.* Plenty of imperial soldiers
would desert if they could remember why they should. Recruitable deserters in
Act II are people whose drill wore thin.

**Heraldry.** An eagle on crimson, "the bird that looks at the sun without
blinking", the empire's claim to have mastered the Hollow Sun. The Emperor's
eagle shield is gilt.

### The Emperor's household

- **Household knights.** Converts of the Unsworn Night and their sons, in
  parade plate "reforged for war". Their crests are older than the empire
  because their families are.
- **The palace guard.** The Roll's own guard. One name on their roster is
  circled in red: *DO NOT ENGAGE ALONE*.
- **The Emperor's last guardians.** Revenant champions wearing "every standard
  the empire ever took, stacked like trophies": the Saltmarch's, the Tarn's
  and the Dry Country's. Their own names were among the first fed.

## The court circle

**What it is.** The mages who went down the Hearth stair with the Marshal on
the Unsworn Night, and their successors. Twelve at a time, by tradition, in a
ring around the Hearthstone, mirroring the Hallow's ring of stones. Chief
among them is the **Archmage**.

**What it does.** It runs the feeding. Its cantors read the Great Roll into
the dark in shifts, day and night, which is what the Eclipse measures. It
teaches dark magic from the Middle Pages to cavalry officers, who come back
"quiet". It sends sealed orders between the ritual sites by the Dark Rider:
lists of which names to call next. Its seals are "on every door" in the capital.

**What it believes.** That the Sleeper can be **steered**. The Archmage has
said so "since long past the hour it could be". The circle treats the Sleeper
as a mechanism with inputs, the Entity, and the war as an experiment. It is
the only imperial institution that openly studies the loop, which it calls
**the rifts**.

**Its texts.** The **Middle Pages** of the liturgy, stolen, annotated and
misread. The academy's library, taken in S 13, behind "a locked door".

## The old kingdom and the Border Crown

**What it was.** A small realm along the Wend (O 1–612), founded on the Oath
at the Ford, ruled by nameless kings who spent themselves at the Hearthstone.
Its name was spent on the Unsworn Night. Everyone calls it "the old kingdom"
because they cannot call it anything else. In old chronicles its name is a
line drawn across the page.

**What it believed.** That every name holds the dark down equally, so the
weakest subject's name matters as much as the king's, and that the crown is a
debt the king pays first.

**What survives of it.**

- **The Border Crown** (S 2–28): the Marches under Wystan the Uncrowned at
  Wendhall. Destroyed in the Second Push. Its heir is Edric.
- **The Oath at the Ford**, said whole by paladins who "recite the oath whole,
  including the parts they cut".
- **The ember**: fire from the Hearthstone, carried from the palace hearth
  to Wendhall to the warband's fire pit.
- **The horn call** (the Old Kingdom motif).
- **Its roads, forts, castles and drills**, all taken over by the empire.
- **Its teal.** Edric wears it; nobody else is entitled to.

**Its kings.** Nameless once crowned, known by the epithets their people gave
them: the King at the Ford, the Lantern Queen, the Short King (the Storm-Year),
the Kind, the Unready, the Gentle King (the last). The border chronicles list
thirty-one.

## The temple of the Dawn

One faith, for everyone, older than the kingdom. It has no priesthood of the
Thread; that is the seers. The temple's work is to **keep names**:

- **Naming.** Children are named aloud at a shrine and the shrine keeps the
  name. "The roadside shrine asks only a copper and a name. It keeps both."
- **The book of returns.** Every parish keeps one. Revival is reading the
  dying person's name back to them until the body remembers its shape. One
  return per soul per season, because the book can only hold a name so
  firmly so often. *SEASON NEGOTIABLE.*
- **Promotion rites.** A new rank is recorded "in careful ink" and the new
  title woven onto the name. Promotions were church business in the old
  kingdom, and the warband's chapels still do them that way.
- **The Vigil of Lamps.** Kindling a chapel's name-lamp and reading its book
  aloud to push back the dark. The game's **Kindle** is this rite.
- **The Dawn-office.** The daily liturgy, a hymn with a rest where her name
  should be. Dawn-office verses turn up in weapon lore (*"Dawn-office, verse
  nine: take back from the foe what the foe took from you."*).

**Since the Defacing (S 9)** it has been two temples:

- **The cathedral** in the Seat, where the bishops who "fled to the capital"
  bless the levy and the Roll. Its statues of the Dawn have no faces.
- **The chaplains**: village chaplains, fen priests, abbesses and abbots of
  the remaining houses, battle monks and a few tricksters, who kept their
  books when the clerks came and still name children in the old way. "The
  shrines stand empty, but the grace does not."

**Iconography.** The Hollow Sun: a black disc with a thin gold ring. Statues of
the Dawn with the face chipped away. A lamp. A cupped hand.

## The seer order

**What it was.** The keepers of the Thread and the Hallow, based at the
chapter-house at the Glass since before the kingdom. Fenfolk by origin.
Cream-robed, so as not to be mistaken for a name. Its head was the **Keeper
of the Last Pages**.

**Its texts.** The liturgy is in three parts:

| Part | Subject | Held by |
|---|---|---|
| **The First Pages** | The Naming: who the Dawn was, why names must be kept | The whole temple; read aloud everywhere |
| **The Middle Pages** | The Keeping: the rites of the Hearthstone, how a king spends his name | The kings, until O 603; now the court circle |
| **The Last Pages** | The Waking: what sleeps, what wakes it, and "the thread by which the world is pulled back" | The Keeper alone. Read aloud only at a priestess's vow. Now Sera. |

The Last Pages end mid-sentence in every copy ever made. "The seers' notes
end mid-sentence here." The sentence breaks off because the rest of it is
sung, and cannot be sung by one voice ([07](07-found-texts.md#the-last-pages)).

**Its fate.** Raided in S 12 (the Taking), burned in S 30. Sera is the last
seer. The order's **lay students** carried the light into the villages and
were called Light Sages. The war cut their studies short.

**The High Commission** (S 5–21) was the empire's imitation: hedge-seers
scrying in black glass shards from the Hearth caldera, escorted by sky-legion
riders. "I flew escort for the commission's seers. None of them saw this."

## Border wardens

**What they are.** The old kingdom's rangers of the western Line, sworn to
**the Line** itself rather than to the king. Their oath survived the Unsworn
Night, so the Marches held for twenty-six more years.

**Two ledgers.** Every warden keeps patrol routes and the dead. The ledger of
the dead is not sentiment. Writing a name in it keeps that name out of the
dark ([Cosmology](01-cosmology.md#how-names-are-kept)). A warden reads the
page aloud on the anniversary. Voss's ledger has run long since S 28.

**Customs.** Moss-green mantles. Snare-books. "Ask the roots nicely." They
have a word for an eclipse, *the Swallow*, which Voss has forgotten; he
remembers only that it meant "the sun and the moon in one stroke".
Wardens do not recruit talkers. "Too chatty, they said."

**Now.** Some are dead, some serve the Iron Captain's garrison, and a few are
still on the Line, unregistered. **The Iron Captain** was a warden-captain.
His title *Warden of the March* is the empire's theft of theirs.

## Smith-saints

An order of smiths sworn at the anvil ("Sworn palm-down on the anvil stone"),
founded to strike makers' names into iron so they keep. Varen was the
greatest of them. They cursed the **Blood Forge** rite, which quenches an
edge in the smith's own name, because it spends what they exist to keep. It
works anyway. They give pilgrims a discount. Varen's line still works the Cut.

## Schools of war

- **The war colleges.** Old-kingdom officer schools, closed in S 2. The border's
  **Ford College** lasted until S 28 and closed the day before Kira's final
  board. The empire's single **Imperial War College** in the Seat teaches
  the *Doctrine of Lances* without the Oath. Board notation on an
  eight-rank board; the Warp staff was banned there because "marching is
  doctrine, not a suggestion". Grandmasters beat the headmaster.
- **The academy.** Old-kingdom school of the Old Tongue in the old capital,
  moved after the Nine Days and burned in S 13. Its graduates are Mages and
  Sages; its expelled are Warlocks. "Expelled for reading past chapter nine."
- **The dueling halls.** In the old kingdom they judged disputes over names:
  inheritances, titles, marriages. The empire kept them as spectacle and
  recruiting ground. Rules: *never strike first, never refuse a challenge;
  ONE AT A TIME, GENTLEMEN; one glove thrown, no seconds, no help.* Each
  hall keeps a "proof", its best, like a master weight. The Blade Lord was
  the seventh.
- **Frontier sword-schools.** Private schools that train on debt. *BE FAST OR
  BE ELSEWHERE.* Myrmidons work off tuition in duels.
- **The berserk school.** Founded by Wat, the Berserker King, at the fens'
  edge. Teaches **the red**: forget your name in battle and fear goes with
  it. It works. The pupils who learn it best never quite remember their
  names again and are chained at night. The school calls that graduation.
  It is the one institution that spends its own names on purpose, and so
  the corruption finds nothing in it to change.

## The sky legions

Founded c. O 150, when a pegasus came down at the Hallow and chose a
reed-cutter's daughter. Pegasi are held to be the Dawn's horses, the ones that
once drew the morning, and they choose their riders ("She chose me over a
captain's heir."). The legions flew pickets over the valley and kept its maps.
From their altitude they knew the realm's outline was a sleeper's
([Cosmology](01-cosmology.md#the-hallow-and-the-hearth)). That is why the
empire first folded them into the High Commission and then disbanded them,
and why a few of their charts are the most dangerous documents in the realm.

**Seraph Knights**: "a few sky lancers flew high enough that the light took
notice." Up there the corona is close.

## The clans

Two peoples, often confused by lowlanders:

**The hill clans (border clans).** South-west of the Marches. Axe-bearing,
kin-reckoning, independent. They keep their dead's names in **blood-song**,
sung at every feast. The Old Treaties (O 43) kept them and the kingdom at
peace for six centuries. Their warchief Skarde broke the Treaties in S 28
under the empire's "new terms", on the reasoning that the Treaties had been
sworn with a kingdom that no longer had a name. The Warchief's card cue has
"the clan's own dignity, not the Empire's brass... a low hum of the clan's
voices."

**The peak clans (mountain clans).** Above the snow line north-east of the
Hearth. Wyvern riders. They "crown no kings", decide by moot and vote, and
settle disputes by letting a wyvern lord's shadow cross the village. They
hold that wyverns hatch from the dreams of the dragons asleep in the Hallow,
and that their own names, given in the Old Tongue, are older than the
kingdom's: "we were here first." The old kings feared them for it. They came
down to fight once, in the Storm-Year. Clan law since: *never fly for a
banner.* They sell wyverns to the empire for salt and goats and a promise
"to keep the beast clear of tower mages". They voted to stay out of this war.
Some of their riders lost the vote and came anyway.

## Guilds, companies and trades

- **The mercenary guild.** Keeps the **Company Books**, a register of sellswords
  and their contracts. A guild-registered sword is under contract, not under
  the Roll, so the levy cannot take them. It is the only legal way to keep
  your name out of the Roll, and the guild charges accordingly. "Don't mention
  this to the guild. My reputation is my wages."
- **The courier guild.** Boots "resoled four times". Its best were never caught
  or paid.
- **Bounty hunters.** Guild brand, notched per head. "The guild pays; the guild
  never asks whose."
- **Troupes and bards.** They smuggle the old songs, which hold names the Roll
  never recorded, "through checkpoints that cannot tax a song". A troupe
  singing the border ballads in an Act IV tavern is committing treason.
- **Deep-wood families.** Hunters who feed the border towns in lean years and
  hand down cloaks and bows.
- **Merchants.** Hagen, mostly ([05](05-dramatis-personae.md#hagen)).

## Partisans

Not an institution. The people of the Roads who scratch numbers off
milestones, chalk *STILL HERE* on alley walls, and pass a signal from hill to
hill: two quick notes and a leap of a seventh, the partisans' motif in *Old
Kingdom Roads*. They have no leader, which is why the empire cannot feed one.
They will house the warband for a night and deny it in the morning.
