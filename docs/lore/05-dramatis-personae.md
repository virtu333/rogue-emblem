# 5 · Dramatis Personae

Everyone who matters, what happened to them before the March, what they
want, and what they are hiding. Voice rules for the lords stay in
[lore-style-guide.md](../lore-style-guide.md#lord-voice-sheet). This file is
their history.

Ages are given as of S 34. Each entry cites the shipped lines its history
explains, so writers can see what is already load-bearing.

---

## The seven lords

### Edric — heir to the crown in the river

*Lord → Great Lord. 22. Teal.*

**Birth.** Born S 12 at Wendhall to **Wystan the Uncrowned**, son of the last
king, and **Idony**, a warden's daughter from Birchwick who died of fever when
Edric was five. Raised in a border hall that was also a court in exile: a
fireplace holding the ember from the Hearth, a banner nobody could fly in the
valley, a gate guard, and a father who refused a crown so he could keep his
name.

**Upbringing.** Banner-house custom: *the lord walks in front and learns every
name behind him.* Wystan made Edric recite the hall's names at supper: every
servant, groom, warden and yeoman. By twelve he knew four hundred. The village
smith at the Cut, **Old Brannoc**, taught him to hold a sword and told him he
would never be the finest blade in the room, "only the one that stays".

**The burning (S 28).** Sixteen. He was in the hall when the Knight
Commander's lances came over the east wall. The gate, where Cael stood, never
opened. His father sent him out the back
with the banner and the ember pot; Brannoc carried him when he wouldn't go.
Two hundred and twelve people died at Wendhall. Edric knows all their names
and says them to himself on the march. That is the "counting".

**The Hiding (S 28–31).** Three years in the hills above the Ford, keeping the
ember alive in a pot and doing nothing else, because nobody would follow him
and he thought nobody should. Then a seer walked out of the east and told him
she had seen him counting.

**What he wants.** To get everyone home. He does not want the crown, and he
has a reason he has never said aloud: in the old way, a king **spends his
name**. His Great Lord line, *"I'd rather they kept calling me Edric, if nobody
minds"*, is not modesty. He is afraid of becoming a man with no name, like
the one on the throne. **(II)**

**What he knows and won't look at.** Everyone on the border knows the ballad:
the Morning Crown went into the Wend at the Ford. His father dragged the
gravel for a year and found nothing, and the river has run slow there ever
since. The warband camps on the hill above it on the night before. Edric has
slept above it on every thread and has never gone down to look, because if he
found it he would have to decide what to do with it. Whether it is still there
is the part nobody knows ([09 § The crown in the Wend](09-channeling.md#the-crown-in-the-wend)).

**The Knight Commander.** Leofric led the Push, and he offered Wendhall terms.
The Dark Rider arrived the same evening with the court circle's sealed orders,
and the hall burned instead. Edric knows this. That is why his reply to
Leofric is "Your soldiers deserve better orders" and not something harder: he
blames the orders.

**Relationships.**
- **Sera** saw a lord in him before he did. He grounds her, and he is the only
  one who tells her she carries too much.
- **Cael** was his father's gate guard. Edric brought him bread at the gate
  when he was a boy. Neither mentions it.
- **Kira** directs, he stands. He trusts her board because she trusts his
  banner.
- **Rowan** mocks his lance work. He laughs. It's the only teasing he gets.
- **Voss** knew his mother. That is why Voss follows a boy.

**Lines this explains.** "I've come a long way from the border hall." ·
"The village smith would laugh to see me now." · "Power stolen from a sleeping
god. We're here to return it." · "The crown the old kingdom lost. I'll try very
hard not to lose it again." At his promotion *it* means what the crown was
for: the debt, and the people it was owed to. He is not putting anything on
his head.

### Sera — the last seer

*Light Sage → Light Priestess. 20. Cream robe, red hair, green eyes.*

**Birth.** Born S 14 on a reed-island in the fens to fenfolk who gave her to
the chapter-house at four, as fen families with a sighted child always had.
In the order's roll she was **Serafen** **(III)**; to everyone, like any
novice, she was Sera.

**The chapter-house.** Raised in the last years of the seer order, which had
been gutted by the Taking six years before she arrived. There were no other
children. The novices who would have been her seniors had been taken to the
Glass in S 12. One of them, **Tamlin**, came back as something else. His name
was still in the novice roll, and she grew up reading it.

**The Thread (S 29).** Fifteen. The Great Feeding reached the Hallow, the Glass
rose black, and the Thread came to her all at once: every road of the next
five years, and a young man at the head of a column, counting. On the far
side of the Glass, the Lieutenant looked up at the same moment and saw her
seeing. Neither has looked away since.

**Graduation (S 30).** The circle came back. The last Keeper graduated her in
one sentence ("You are the order now"), put the **Last Pages** and the
**novice roll** in her hands, and sent her west. A sky lancer found her on
the causeway and carried her out.

**What she carries.**
- **The Thread.** The last of the Dawn's sight. She has walked this March more
  times than she will say.
- **The Last Pages**, which she is now the only one allowed to read aloud.
  "The last pages of the liturgy are mine to keep now."
- **Her vow**: "What sleeps, and what wakes it. I swore to both."
- **The weight.** "Ten turns of the weave. I remember every one. I'd rather
  not."

**What it costs (III, sealed).** Every thread she re-weaves costs her some of
her own name. The long form went first: by now even she has trouble
remembering there was more than *Sera*. She has not told anyone and does not
intend to. What is left will be spent if the March goes on
long enough. The thing she fears most is not dying. It is finishing the March
as someone nobody can call.

**The Lieutenant.** Her rival, her mirror, and the boy whose name she has
read a thousand times. She sees the faces of threads; he sees their undersides.
She wants to say his name to him once. The end of the Lieutenant's boss theme,
where "Sera's solo violin plays the Lieutenant's falling answer once, with no
shadow: she sees his future too", is her doing exactly that. Given back his
name, he has a thread of his own for the first time since he was seven, and
she can see it ([09](09-channeling.md#31--names-returned)).

**Relationships.**
- **Edric**: "You never needed a vision, Edric. You only needed them."
- **Astrid** carried her out of the fens. They rarely talk about it.
- **Hagen** remembers the long form of her name. The one time he says it,
  it frightens her.

### Kira — the board

*Tactician → Grandmaster. 24. Plum coat.*

**Birth.** Born S 10 in the Fourth Province, a toll-keeper's daughter who
counted carts from the toll-house window before she could read. By eight she
could tell the tolls by the ruts.

**The Ford College (S 22–28).** A partisan schoolmaster smuggled her west at
twelve on a forged pass, to the last free war college, at Oathford. She was
the best student of its last fifteen years and the most insufferable. The
board is an eight-rank grid; the colleges taught that "where is the enemy you
cannot see?" is answered in board notation. Her final board, to be named
Grandmaster by beating the headmaster, was set for the morning after the
college closed. The headmaster, **Master Orrin Tace**, died in the Second Push
that week.
She took his chair's worth of captured imperial ledgers west in a cart.

**What she found.** Three years of reading imperial rolls against imperial
casualty tables. The Roll is **shrinking faster than the dead can explain**.
Names are leaving it without bodies. She was the first person outside the
court circle to prove the feeding, with arithmetic. This is what the GDD
meant by "studied the corruption's patterns".

**What she knows (II).** Extrapolated, the feeding reaches its sum by
**midwinter S 34**, this winter. The March has a deadline and she knows the
date. The Eclipse is the part of that arithmetic everyone else can see. It is
why she says "I hate endings": she can see exactly when this one is.

**Relationships.**
- **Edric**: "Your banner, my board." She thinks he is the only piece on the
  board that can't be traded, and has told no one.
- **Rowan**: she claims credit for his luck. She is half right.
- **Sera**: exasperation. A seer who won't share her sight is, to Kira, a
  player hiding a piece.
- **The Knight Commander**: she learned cavalry from his *Doctrine* and has
  been waiting six years to say "I have read your cavalry doctrine" to his face.

### Voss — the ledger

*Ranger → Vanguard. 44. Moss mantle, greatsword, bow on his back.*

**Birth.** Born S −10 (O 602) in the Third Province, a tenant farmer's son,
two years before the Unsworn Night. He grew up in the empire and was entered
in the Roll as a child. At twenty-four, in S 14, when the levy came for his
village a second time, he walked west with his friend **Anders Carrow** to the
one place the Roll could not reach. They were sworn as wardens of the Line
the same week. Wardens swear to the Line and not to any name, so no Roll can
own the oath.

**The Line (S 14–34).** Twenty years on the border. He married **Nessa**, a
birch-weaver of Birchwick, and they had one daughter, **Tilde**. He rose to
senior warden of the western Line, and he says so when he says "Power, huh.
Had that once. Outlived it." His sworn brother through all of it was Carrow,
who became warden-captain at Oathford.

**The burning (S 28).** Voss was on patrol in the hills, holding the clans back
through a gap the Old Treaties had covered, when the Push came through the
woods behind him. He got home to Birchwick three days after it burned. He has
been writing in the ledger since. "My family's names are in my ledger."

**Carrow.** While Voss was in the hills, Carrow surrendered the Ford and gave
his name to the Roll: the thing the two of them had walked west to escape. Voss has written **Anders Carrow** in the ledger of the
dead, though Carrow is alive and commands the border. He has not decided
whether that was mercy or judgment. When he says "Twenty years here is long
enough" to the Iron Captain, he is speaking to his brother.

**Tilde.** She plaited charms from birch bark and her own hair. The
**Forest Charm** in the game ("Plaited by a warden's daughter from birch and
hair. The trees know her knot.") is hers **(II)**. Voss has never seen one
since the burning and does not know any survived.

**The old wardens' word.** "The sun and the moon in one stroke. The old
wardens had a word for it. I forget." The word was *the Swallow*, the wardens'
name for an eclipse. He remembers it at level 20 ([09](09-channeling.md)).

### Rowan — the blessed lance

*Chevalier → Holy Knight. 26. Gray-muzzled mare.*

**Birth.** Born S 8 at the remount studs of the Sixth Province, where his
family had bred cavalry horses for the old kingdom for six generations and
now bred them for the empire.

**The lance-master.** A cashiered old-kingdom household knight who kept a
paddock near the studs and would not say his name. He had refused the struck
oath and had nowhere else to go. He taught Rowan the lance, and the whole
Oath, fourth line and all, from age nine. In S 26, when Rowan was eighteen,
the old man took him to a ruined temple and blessed his lance there. Then he
went home to his paddock, where he still is.

**What the blessing was (II).** No chaplain was present. The old man spent
his own name into the lance, the old way: the strong serve the weak. He has
not had a name since, and finds this funny. The lance is blessed because a
man's name is in it. Rowan thinks the blessing was a formality
and his aim is his own. He is right about his aim.
"I used to joke about the blessing. I think it was listening. Thank you,
whoever."

**The mare.** His sister's, bred from his family's line: gray at the muzzle,
fast for her age. Rowan never says her name
aloud where anyone can hear, "so the Roll can't have her". He whispers it
into her ear. It is **Bess** **(II)**.

**The Knight Commander.** The lance-master had been the Knight Commander's
shield-brother in the Gentle King's household. On the Unsworn Night one found
faith in the Emperor and the other found a paddock. "Let us see the empire's
first lance at work" is the pupil meeting his master's brother without
knowing it.

**Why he rode west.** S 32: a remount inspection took his sister's name for
the levy. He took nine deserters and her mare and rode for the border.

### Astrid — the far picket

*Sky Lancer → Seraph Knight. 23. Winged circlet after promotion.*

**Birth.** Born S 11 among the **peak clans**, in a wyvern-riding family,
which is the whole problem. A wild pegasus came down in the clan's high meadow
when she was eight and chose her. Clans ride wyverns; a pegasus is the Dawn's
horse, a lowland thing. She was politely asked to leave.

**The legions.** At fourteen (S 25) she joined the legion remnants that had
flown west to the Border Crown after the High Commission was dissolved. "The sky
legions called me hopeless in formation." They were right, because clan
children do not fly in lines. They sent her to the far pickets of the Marches:
"fly alone, keep your distance, report what the ground can't see." She
reported to Wendhall. After S 28 there was nobody to report to. She kept flying the picket anyway.

**The causeway (S 30).** She saw a girl in cream walking west on the Stillwater
causeway with the circle's riders behind her, and landed.

**What she has seen (II).** The legions' old charts are in her saddlebag. From
the far pickets, high enough, she has seen the land's outline: the Wend, the
fens and the Hearth making the shape of something asleep on its side. "The
wind dies at the same stones every time." She has told only Sera. Sera
already knew.

**Voice note.** Clipped because she was raised to talk to wyverns, which don't
answer, and then flew alone for six years.

### Cael — the gate

*Sentinel → Champion. 27. Kettle helm, crimson scarf, cross tabard.*

**Birth.** Born S 7, a quarryman's son from the Cut who was too big for the
quarry and too patient for the levy. He joined the Wendhall guard at fifteen.

**The order (S 25).** Wystan the Uncrowned posted him to the hall gate
himself: *hold this gate until relieved*. Wystan kept his name, so an oath
sworn by it held **(II)**. When the Push came in S 28, Cael held the gate
until there was nothing behind it to hold. Then he kept holding it, because
nobody had relieved him.

**Five years (S 28–33).** He lived in the gatehouse of a burned hall. He
repaired the gate twice. Imperial patrols learned to go around. "They posted
me at a gate that fell years ago. The order still stands. So do I." The hall
fell; the gate, strictly, did not. He is precise about this.

**The scarf.** Crimson, the old red of the Border Crown's gate guard, which the
empire later took for its ink. He will not change it. "It was ours first."

**Why he follows Edric (II).** Wystan is dead. Only Wystan's heir can relieve
him, and to do it Edric would have to say the words and the watchword, which
Edric doesn't know. So Cael follows the heir, keeping his post in the only way
still possible. "The old kingdom never rescinded my orders. I've decided they
meant this." He does not want to be relieved and has no intention of telling
Edric the watchword. The watchword is *ember* **(III)**.

**The Iron Wall.** "I know what it costs to hold alone." Two men who held a
post past all sense: one for a king who kept faith with him, one for a king
whose order never came.

## The antagonists

Imperial officers surrendered their names to the Roll on commission. The
names below are the ones they had before. Game text should almost never use
them. The exception is the name-return moment ([09](09-channeling.md#31--names-returned)).

### The Iron Captain — Anders Carrow

*Warden of the Unrelieved Line. Act I. Cavalier. About 50.*

A tenant farmer's son from the Third Province who walked west with Voss in
S 14 to get out of the Roll. A border warden from that year and
warden-captain at Oathford from S 22. Voss's sworn brother. In the summer of S 28 he held the Ford eleven weeks against
the Knight Commander while waiting for relief from Wendhall that never came,
because Wendhall was fighting the clans. When his wardens were boiling
harness leather, he surrendered on terms: his garrison would live, stay on
the border and be exempt from the levy. The price was his name. He was made
**Warden of the March**. He signs his orders with an iron stamp.

He eats with his troops. He mends his own banner. He is still waiting for an
order from Wendhall; Sera can see him waiting. His music keeps returning to D
where the Empire's falls away: "duty, not ambition." He is the most decent
man the warband kills, and they kill him first.

**Memory.** He dreams the loop and calls it bad sleep.
**Defeat.** "The border was supposed to hold." It was his oath, once.
**Name returned.** *Anders Carrow, captain of wardens at Oathford. He held the
Ford eleven weeks. Relief never came.*

### The Warchief — Skarde of the hill clans

*Breaker of the Old Treaties. Act I. Fighter. About 45.*

Not an imperial officer and not on the Roll. Hill-clan custom: a warchief
lays down his name for the length of a war and is called only *Warchief*; if
he dies, his clan sings his name at the feast. In spring S 28 he broke the
six-hundred-year Old Treaties, on the reasoning that a treaty sworn with a
kingdom whose name is spent is sworn with no one, and took the empire's "new
terms". His clans get the lowland pastures the Marches no longer hold. He
despises the empire and says so; he despises the old kingdom for letting its
name be taken; he enjoys a fight more than either.

**Defeat.** "Hah... you fight well." He means it. His clan sings *Skarde* that
night, and the warband can hear it from the hills.

### The Knight Commander — Leofric of the Household

*First Lance of the Second Push. Act II. Paladin. 59. Silver hair, gold of rank.*

A household knight of the Gentle King, posted to the Hearth hold in O 611 with
a few others to keep an eye on the disgraced Marshal. On the Unsworn Night he
woke unsworn and found he felt nothing at all: no loyalty, no shame, no reason
to stand. The Marshal, coming up the stair at dawn, looked at him and gave him
an order. The relief of it was so great that Leofric has called it faith ever
since. He fought for the empire at the Nine Days against men he had eaten
with. His shield-brother, who later taught Rowan, went the other way.

He wrote the *Doctrine of Lances* (S 15), still taught at the Imperial War
College, and he has read the casualty tables it cost. He led the Second Push.
He offered Wendhall terms; the Dark Rider brought the circle's orders that
evening, and Leofric burned it anyway. He has not forgiven the orders or
himself. He kept the gold trim of his old household livery
through the empire's colour change. The warband's artists noticed.

**Secret (II).** He still knows the Oath's fourth line. He hums it.
**Memory.** "I have died for the emperor in my nightmares." He files the
nightmares under faith.
**Name returned.** *Leofric, household knight to the Gentle King. He knew the
fourth line. He hummed it.*

### The Archmage — Cyprian Vantrell

*Keeper of the Middle Pages. Act II. Sage. 63. White beard, red-orb staff.*

An academy prodigy who at twenty found a mis-shelved copy of the Middle Pages
in the royal archive, stole it, and read it against the grain. He brought it to the Lord Marshal in O 603. He
led the circle down the stair on the Unsworn Night, burned the academy in
S 13 and took its library, and has run the feeding since S 25. He is the
court circle's first voice and its most honest: he thinks the Sleeper can be
steered, he has thought so since long past the hour it could be, and he is
curious rather than cruel.

**The notebooks (II).** He is the one enemy who writes the loop down. He
calls cut threads *rifts* and has kept a notebook on the warband's "visits"
since the rifts began: dates, positions, causes of death. It is the most
accurate record of the player's failed runs that exists in the world. The
paper does not survive a cut thread, of course. He rewrites the whole thing
at the start of every thread from what he calls very specific dreams, and it
is uncannily accurate. He cannot explain this and it delights him. "So the
rifts do loop. I kept notes on your last visit... and on how you die in this
one."
**Defeat.** "Reproducible... my death is... reproducible... what a final datum."
**Name returned.** *Cyprian Vantrell, of the academy. He read the Middle Pages
against the grain, and was right, and it did not help.*

### The Dark Rider — the second rider

*Bearer of the Sealed Orders. Act II. Dark Knight.*

A mantle, not a man. The court circle's sealed orders are lists of names to be
read next. They travel by one courier on a black horse between the ritual
sites. Opening the bag unnames the reader, so the riders are chosen from men
with nothing left to lose. The roads empty ahead of him because people know
what he carries.

- **The first rider** was **Jory Rusk**, the Gentle King's courier at the
  Nine Days, who took the Marshal's money to be late with the order to fall
  back. He never delivered it and never threw it away. Rider from S 0 until
  his death in S 11.
- **The second and current rider** is **Piers Rusk**, Jory's son, who
  inherited the mantle, a black horse of the same line, the bag, and in the
  bag, never delivered, **the Gentle King's order to fall back** **(III,
  earned)**. He has never opened it.

He hears the Sleeper through the lists as whispers, and he calls it "the
dark". It tells him a rider falls at this crossroads. It has told him that on
every thread, and he has always assumed it meant someone else. "Always this
crossroads... always your blade... the dark... lied to me."
**Name returned.** *Piers Rusk, son of the courier who sat on a king's order.
He carried it twenty-three years and never opened the bag.*

### The Blade Lord — Tiernan Vale

*Proof of the Dueling Halls. Act III. Swordmaster. 40. Pale, long black hair.*

The dueling halls' seventh **proof**, the swordsman a hall keeps as its
standard of perfect form. Never touched in formal combat. In S 16 the empire
assigned him to guard and train the eleven-year-old Lieutenant. Within two
years the boy was choosing his duels for him: foreseeing each, sealing the
paths where Tiernan lost. He has never lost because he has never fought a
duel the Lieutenant did not choose. He calls the boy *master* and loves him
like a younger brother. The warband's Myrmidons know him as the duelist who
crippled a sword-school master in the Third Province in S 20.

**Why he fights.** The Lieutenant promised him he would never be touched. "The
lieutenant said this path was sealed." On the fens, which "keep no forms",
the promise stops holding.
**Defeat.** "Master, I'm sorry." He means the Lieutenant.
**Name returned.** *Tiernan Vale, seventh proof of the halls. Never touched in
formal combat. The fens keep no forms.*

### The Iron Wall — Bertil Stane

*Holder of the Breach. Act III. General. 56. Great helm, striped tower shield.*

A royal knight of the Gentle King at twenty-two. At the Nine Days he held the
Breach in the old capital's north wall with forty men for a day and a half
after the city had fallen around him, because the order to fall back never
came. It was in Jory Rusk's bag. The Marshal took him alive and offered him
the empire's walls. Unsworn, with nothing left to be loyal to but the act of
holding, Bertil accepted. "The empire made his stand its doctrine."

**Why he fights.** The Lieutenant showed him a future in which his walls are
never breached. It is the only promise he has ever been made that did not
come from a dead king.
**What he never received.** His order. Sera can see it: "I see the courier you
never heard arrive" ([09 § The Unopened Order](09-channeling.md#the-unopened-order)).
**Defeat.** "The walls... are nothing... without the will behind them."
**Name returned.** *Bertil Stane, of the Gentle King's household. He held the
Breach a day and a half past the order to leave. The order has arrived.*

### The Berserker King — Wat

*Crowned by Frightened Acclaim. Act III. Berserker. About 54.*

A Grey Winter orphan who drove salt carts on the Roads, pressed at nineteen
into the Marshal's baggage train, and put into the storming party at the
Breach when the Marshal ran short of men. Bertil Stane's forty
killed everyone around him. Wat forgot his own name that afternoon in a red
fog, and found he could no longer be afraid. He spent ten years teaching
deserters to do the same on purpose, which is the red, and in S 15 his pupils
crowned him because nobody dared not to.

**Why the corruption changed nothing.** He spent his own name decades ago;
there is nothing left in him for the Sleeper to take. The score: "he is
unchanged, only more so."
**Why he fights.** The Lieutenant came to him in S 26 and described a death
worth having. "The worthy death from my dreams."
**The name nobody holds.** His name was never fed; he threw it away, so the
Sleeper doesn't have it and the choir will not sing it. **Only Hagen remembers
it**, from the salt run forty years ago ([09](09-channeling.md#31--names-returned)).
**Name returned (only by Hagen).** *Wat. Drove the salt run on the Roads. Good
with mules. Laughed at everything.*

### The Emperor — the Marshal

*Who Sold the Empire's Future. Act IV. General. 79. Crown, gold plate, eagle shield.*

A miller's son from the grain country, first of his year at the war college, the Grey Winter's
hero: the Lord Marshal who moved grain through snow for three years and
watched the dead go into the chaplains' books by the thousand. He decided the
Oath was a luxury the kingdom could no longer afford and the Great Vigil a
prayer where a plan was needed. On the Unsworn Night he fed his own name
first, then the kingdom's, then the condemned. He heard the hum. He has heard it every day since.

**What he believes.** That he has held the dark back for thirty-four years,
which is true: fed, the Sleeper stayed quiet. That he can hold it forever,
which is false, because each feeding made it heavier and nearer. That when it
turns, it will turn toward him, and he will rule a world where nothing
changes. He is "human, powerful, and wrong".

**The gold.** He wears gold, the player's colour, because he has hoarded names.
The court circle reserves a tithe of every feeding for him. It is "what I
kept for myself". It is why he is still alive at seventy-nine, and why the
Sleeper is "nothing between you and—" once he falls.

**Hard's thread (II).** When the Lieutenant's champions start falling and the
warband is still coming, the Emperor spends his own seer to finish the sum.
He then tells the warband they killed him. "You think you've won something by
killing my seer?" He may even believe it ([08](08-reconciliation.md#the-three-routes)).

**Name.** Gone. He fed it first, and it is at the bottom.

### The Lieutenant — Tamlin

*From the Far Side of the Glass. Final boss (Normal). Hero. 29. Scarred face, crimson-lined black plate.*

A fen child given to the chapter-house at four, like Sera after him. In S 12,
aged seven, he was taken with the other novices in the Taking, when the court
circle meant to send a seer through the Glass to the far side and fetch back
sight from underneath. He is the one who came home: no name, a face scarred by
something in the water, and eyes that saw every future that ended. Game text
keeps the Taking offstage. It is enough that he was taken young. The circle's records call him "the first success" and "the first
casualty".

He is not cruel. He is a boy who has been able to see the end of every
possible thing since he was seven. He has no thread of his own: a person with
no name appears only in other people's threads, never at the center of one,
so he cannot see where he himself is going. What he can see is everyone
else's endings. He seals paths the way a man shuts doors in a burning house.
He believes he has been saving the empire from its own futures for eighteen
years, and he is so tired.

**Who saved him (III, sealed).** The circle didn't bring him back. **Hagen**
did. He went into the Glass after the child and came up with him on the
landing under the Hearth, drowning for the second time in his life to do it.

**Sera.** He has watched her since the moment she first saw the Thread. He
knows she has his name. The fen shrine bells ring it: "Twice, then silence.
Then twice.": *Tam-lin* **(III, earned)**. He has never let himself hear them.
**Defeat.** "Every timeline... you find me in every one..."
**Name returned (only by Sera).** *Tamlin, novice of the chapter-house at the
Glass. Still in the roll.*

### The Entity — the Sleeper

Not a person. See [01 § The Stillness](01-cosmology.md#the-stillness). It has
no name to return; nobody ever gave it one. It is the only thing in the game
that could be *given* a name, and nobody knows what would happen.

## At the edges

### Hagen

*Merchant. 59. Cart, barge, off-key hymns.*

A fen carter's son, born O 587. In the Grey Winter, at twelve, he fell through
the ice of the Glass, the only time it has ever frozen, and three days later
walked out of the Hearth, a long way east, "talking about customers". He drove
the chapter-house's cart for most of his life. In S 12 he went into the Glass
after a novice in the Taking, drowned for the second time, and came up on the
landing under the Hearth with the child. "Drowned in the
fen, I did. Twice, I think."

Since then he has been **unstuck**. He walks threads sideways. He has been on
more of them than Sera has, which is why he is always ahead and why nobody
sees the cart pass. He remembers what you bought. "You always buy the same
things." He knows everyone's full names, including Sera's.

He hums the Dawn-office off-key **(III)**. He is not off key. He is singing
the note that isn't in the hymn, the Root, which nobody has heard in so long
that it sounds wrong. What he is, after two drownings in a crater full of a
goddess's sight, is a [deliberate mystery](01-cosmology.md#deliberate-mysteries).
He will not say. He is running a business.

**Rules for Hagen.** Always cheerful. Never explains. Knows too much, says a
little of it, and charges for it. The loop is funny to him. He is the only
character allowed to be openly loop-aware in common speech.

### Varen

Smith-saint of the Cut, O 404–471. See [the chronicle](02-chronicle.md#varen-o-404--471).
Plain iron, made honest. *A MARK FOR EVERY MAKER.*

### Old Brannoc

The smith at the Cut in S 34, of Varen's line, about seventy. Taught Edric the
sword. Carried him out of Wendhall. "Old Brannoc says a crooked blade is just
a club with opinions." He is too old to march and stayed at the Cut. He would
laugh to see Edric now. Then he'd check his grip.

### Old Hessa

Drill-mistress of the western Line at Birchwick for thirty years. "Taught forty
recruits in [the Mentor's Band]. Thirty-one lived. She called it a good year."
She taught Voss, Carrow and Tilde. She was not at Birchwick when it burned.
Where she is now is up to the team. A good candidate for a recruitable special
character.

### Wystan the Uncrowned

The Gentle King's son (O 590–S 28). Carried his dying father to the Ford and
lost the crown in the river. Kept his name, refused the title, and held the
Border Crown at Wendhall for twenty-six years around a fire from the Hearth.
Died in his hall in the Second Push. He posted Cael. He taught Edric the
names.

### The Gentle King

The last king of the old kingdom (crowned O 571, died S 0). A kind, slow,
careful man who went down to the Hearthstone twice. He refused the Marshal's
plan and was right, then refused to strike first and lost everything. He
died in the river at the Ford where the first king swore. Nobody knows his
name; he gave it up at twenty-five.

### Cenwyn, the Queen's Gate-Warden

Held the east gate of the old capital nine days with the royal blade later
called **Ragnarok**, and "held it through the night". Died on the tenth
morning, when the gate came down on her. The *Last Bastion* scroll is her drill, written down by a survivor.

### The Lantern Queen

Sister of the twin princes, queen after the Twins' War (O 338). Went down to
the Hearthstone carrying a lamp. The temple's Vigil of Lamps copies her.

### Rhun and Rhodri

The twin princes of the Twins' War (O 331–338), each determined to be the one
to pay the crown's debt. **Gemini** was forged for them to share. Both died on
the bridge of the old capital to one stroke of the **Brave Sword**.

### Jory Rusk

The Gentle King's courier at the Nine Days, paid by the Marshal to be late
with the order to fall back. Kept it anyway. The first Dark Rider. Died S 11.

### Mother Beatrix, the last Keeper

Keeper of the Last Pages until S 30. Graduated Sera in one sentence and stayed
behind at the chapter-house when the circle came.

### Master Orrin Tace

Headmaster of the Ford College. Died in the Second Push the week the college
closed, days before he would have sat across the board from Kira.

### Ansel and Mira

A student at the academy in its last years and the person who wrote to him:
"Ansel, the ring hums while I study. Tell me that's normal. Love, Mira."
Whether Ansel survived the burning in S 13 is left open. The recruit pool has
a mage by that name; a recruit Ansel wearing the Magic Ring is a coincidence
players are welcome to enjoy. Don't make him a fixed character while the
pool name exists.
