excerpted, or used as the source a line is "found" in. The voice rules in
[lore-style-guide.md](../lore-style-guide.md) still apply: item lore ≤ 85

## The Oath at the Ford

Sworn in the river by the first king and the barrow-lords, O 1. Said at every
coronation, every war-college graduation and every paladin's vow until S 2,
and in the Marches until S 28.

> *The strong serve the weak.*
> *Every name is kept.*
> *The crown is a debt, and the king pays first.*
> *What sleeps is not woken, and what wakes it is not suffered.*

**The paladin oath**, copied from it in the second century:

> *I am strength to the line.*
> *I am shield to the banner.*
> *I am service to the crown.*
> *The strong serve the weak.*

The Edict of the Roll struck the fourth line in S 2. Paladins now say three
lines and hum the fourth to the old tune. Paladin recruits who "recite the
oath whole" are committing a small treason every morning.

## The liturgy

Three parts. The First Pages were read aloud in every shrine. The Middle
Pages were for kings. The Last Pages were for the Keeper alone.

### The First Pages (the Naming)

> Before her, nothing came next.
> She asked the dark a question, and the dark had no answer,
> so she was the answer, and the answer was morning.

> She named the fire, and it burned. She named the wind, and it went.
> She named the first great ones, and they flew, and they remember.

> Name the child aloud, that the child may be called back.

> What is named may return. What is kept is not lost.

> When the dark turned over, she had nothing left to give but her name,
> and she paid it out. Look up. The rim is what is left.

### The Dawn-office

The daily hymn. Each verse ends with a rest where her name would be sung.
Verses are numbered and quoted by number.

| Verse | Text |
|---|---|
| one | We keep the lamp because she kept the morning. |
| four | Name the dead before you bury them, or the ground will. |
| seven | The weak are not a burden. The weak are the weight that holds the cloth down. |
| nine | Take back from the foe what the foe took from you. *(shipped, weapon lore)* |
| twelve | Once more, and then once more after that. *(the healer's verse)* |
| twenty | A copper and a name. The shrine keeps both. |
| thirty | Lift them, and let me not be late again. |
| last | *(the choir stops; one bell)* |

### The Middle Pages (the Keeping)

The rites of the Hearthstone. Now held by the court circle, with the
Archmage's marginalia. His notes are in brackets.

> The king goes down alone and comes up the crown.

> Lay the name upon the stone as a mother lays a cloth upon a sleeper.
> Do not throw it. *[Why not? — V.]*

> It will take what is laid. *[Any name? — V.]* *[Any name. Tested. — V.]*

> It will give back what is given unwilling. The king's is given willing,
> and so it gives nothing back, and so the king is safe.
> *[The king is safe. The king is also poor. — V.]*

> What sleeps is not woken. *[Unless it is steered. — V., O 603]*
> *[It can still be steered. — V., S 30]* *[It can still be — V., S 34]*

### The Last Pages (the Waking)

Read aloud only at a priestess's vow. Every copy ever made ends mid-sentence.

> What sleeps below does not hate. It is only before.

> It cannot be fought by one, nor sung down by one.

> In the liturgy's margin, in a shaking hand: *do not wake it to kill it.*

> The thread by which the world is pulled back is not held by the seer.
> The seer holds only the end of it.

> Where did her name go? Look at the child. Look at the one beside you.

> And when all her names are said at once, not by one mouth but by

*(The page ends. In every copy the rest of the leaf is blank and has never been
cut away. The sentence was not lost. It is finished in the finale.)*

## Imperial documents

### The Edict of the Roll (S 2), excerpt

> BY THE SEAT. Every soul within the Empire shall be entered in the Roll,
> and the name so entered is held in trust for the Seat. A name not entered is
> not held. A soul not held is not protected.
>
> Officers of the Seat shall surrender name upon commission and be known by
> rank. The name so surrendered is struck and kept.
>
> The provinces shall be known by number.
>
> WHAT IS, REMAINS.

### Curfew notice

> NO LIGHT AFTER THE NOON BELL SAVE BY WARRANT. A LAMP UNWARRANTED IS A ROGUE
> LIGHT. IT SHALL BE PUT OUT AND THE HOUSEHOLD ENTERED FOR THE LEVY.
>
> *(Pasted over an older notice, whose last line still shows: AFTER DUSK.)*

*(The shipped Act IV line has the two notices the other way round. See
[08 § Recommended edits](08-reconciliation.md#recommended-edits).)*

### Broadsheet, nailed up at Oathford, S 34

> A ROGUE DAWN IN THE WEST. The Seat warns all loyal households of a band of
> rogues marching east under a lost banner and lit against the curfew.
> Do not shelter them. Do not name them. Report all rogue light.

*(This is where the warband's name comes from. Someone pulled the sheet off
the toll-house door the first week, and the soldiers have used the phrase
since.)*

### Commission (the Master Seal)

> COMMISSION. BY THE SEAT.
> The bearer is raised to the rank of ______________.
> Name surrendered: ______________.
> Witnessed, for the Roll: ______________.

### The Lieutenant's letters

Every letter the Lieutenant sends is one word long.

> HOLD. · WAIT. · NOT YET. · NOW. · DON'T.

The last is the only one he ever sent to the Blade Lord.

### The March's crossing order

> CROSSING CLOSED BY ORDER OF THE MARCH.
> *(An iron stamp: a hollow circle. No signature.)*

### Dueling-hall rules

> 1. Never strike first.
> 2. Never refuse a challenge.
> 3. One at a time, gentlemen.
> 4. One glove thrown, one opponent, no seconds, no help.
> 5. The loser's name is the winner's to say or to keep. *(struck by the Seat, S 2)*

### Smith-saint vow

> A mark for every maker. Iron for the keeping.
> Nothing I make goes out nameless.
> Sworn palm-down on the anvil stone.

### Sky-legion picket orders

> Fly alone. Keep your distance.
> Report what the ground can't see.
> If from the height you see the shape, say nothing, and come down slowly.

### The Archmage's rift notebook, excerpts

> merchant? No record. Find out. *[Could not. — V.]*

> *Rift 57.* Died at my hands. I felt, for the first time, the arithmetic of

## Songs and rhymes

### Counting rhyme (the Marches)

> One for the river and two for the ford,
> three for the smith who marked the sword,
> four for the shrine and the copper you paid,
> five for your name, so it won't be mislaid.

### Skipping rhyme (everywhere)

> Hollow sun, hollow sun, where did your name go?
> Down to the babies, down to the snow.
> Say it at morning and say it at night,
> and the rim round the sun will keep its light.

*(Tier I folk rhyme carrying a tier III truth. Nobody who sings it believes
it.)*

### The Crown in the Wend (border ballad)

> The king came down on the ninth night's water,
> the arrow in his side and his son beside;
> he asked to be laid where the first king swore it,
> and the Wend ran slow, and the Wend ran wide.
>
> The crown went down where the gravel's golden,
> and nobody's looked, and nobody's tried,
> for the March has closed it by order in iron,
> and the Wend runs slow, and the Wend runs wide.

### The imperial drill chant

> Left, and the line. Right, and the line.
> What is, remains. What is, remains.

*(Sung on D and E♭. After enough years recruits stop hearing the words.)*

### Levy drinking song

> Scrip for the soldier and scrip for his wife,
> scrip for the name and scrip for the life;
> when the Seat comes to pay what the Seat has to pay,
> it'll pay you in scrip, and it'll pay you in grey.

### Blood-song of the hill clans (the last verse)

> Sing them or lose them. Sing them by name.
> The hill keeps the singer, the song keeps the same.

### The partisans' signal

Two quick notes on the same pitch, a leap up a minor seventh, then a fall. It
has no words. It means *still here*.
