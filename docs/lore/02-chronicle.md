# 2 · The Chronicle

The history of the land, from before time to the night before the March.

**Dating.** The old kingdom counted years **of the Oath** (O), from the
swearing at the Ford. The empire counts years **of the Seat** (S), from the
Unsworn Night. **S 0 = O 612.** The March takes place in **S 34 (O 646)**.
Imperial documents use S dates only; border folk, chaplains and anyone
stubborn use O dates. Dates before the Oath are the chroniclers' guesses,
and the chroniclers admit it.

## Before the chronicles

### The Stillness and the Morning

The liturgy begins before the world: *"Before her, nothing came next. She
asked the dark a question, and the dark had no answer, so she was the answer,
and the answer was morning."*

Nothing is known about the Morning except that it was long and bright and
that everything in it had a name. The dragons were first-named and they
remembered it. Their breath is the Old Tongue, and to this day a peak-clan
elder will call a dragon "older than her own voice".

### The Starfall

Then the Sleeper turned over. The First Pages do not say why. The storms of
that turning are remembered in every people's stories. So is the night that
followed, when the stars came down.

The Dawn spent her name to hold the world together. Fenfolk say she knelt in
the lowland to do it and the ground opened where she knelt, which is the
Glass. Hill folk say the falling lights were her change, and every cradle got
a coin. Peak clans say the dragons lay down around the place so nothing could
dig it up. The liturgy says all three are true.

The morning after, the sun rose with a black heart. It has done so ever
since. The Long Dusk had begun.

### The Long Dusk

Ages pass unrecorded. What the chroniclers can reconstruct:

- **The fenfolk** lived on the reed-islands around the Glass and learned not
  to fish it. Some of their children could see things in it that had not
  happened yet. Those children became the **first seers**. What they saw
  through was the Dawn's unspent sight, which had fallen into the Glass with
  the place she knelt. They called it **the Thread**. The first chapter-house
  was a reed hall at the water's edge. The **First Pages** of the liturgy
  were their teaching: the Naming. Every child must be named aloud, and every
  name must be kept.
- **The barrow kings** ruled the valley of the Wend: petty lords buried under
  mounds with their arms. The Brave Axe came out of one of those mounds,
  from beside one king and two sets of armor. The chronicle has never
  settled who wore the second.
- **The hill clans** (the *border clans*) kept to the south-western hills
  and reckoned kin by blood-song, singing their dead by name at every feast.
- **The peak clans** (the *mountain clans*) lived above the snow line in the
  north-east and tended what the dragons dream: wyverns, which hatch in the
  high nests in winters when the stones of the Hallow stir. The clans crowned
  no kings and never have.

## The Old Kingdom (O 1 – 612)

### The Oath at the Ford (O 1)

The barrow-lords of the Wend were at war over a crossing. The chronicle calls
it only **the Ford**; the border calls it **Oathford** now. A lord whose name
the chronicle does not keep met the others there. Two riders from each side
fought in the river, and "each rider struck twice and the river ran slow"
(the Brave Lance). Then, instead of claiming the crossing, he blew a horn and
asked them to swear.

**The Oath at the Ford** (full text in [07](07-found-texts.md#the-oath-at-the-ford)):

> *The strong serve the weak.*
> *Every name is kept.*
> *The crown is a debt, and the king pays first.*
> *What sleeps is not woken, and what wakes it is not suffered.*

He had been to the chapter-house at the Glass. The seers had shown him what
the land lay on and where its heart was: a warm mountain in the east. He
led his sworn companions there and down into its roots, to a hall where the
stone was warm and beat like a slow drum. That was **the Hearthstone**. He
went in alone and spent his name there as a lullaby, and came out the first
king. He never had a name again, and neither did any king after him. The
crown was the debt, and the king paid first.

His companions carved something on the stair going down. The chronicle says
they carved **WE CAME THIS FAR ALSO**. Masons who have seen the stair say the
letters are older than the Oath ([Mysteries](01-cosmology.md#deliberate-mysteries)).

The horn call he blew at the Ford is the one the score calls **the Old
Kingdom** motif. Border children still hum it without knowing why.

### The kingdom at its height (O 1 – 580)

The kingdom was never large and never conquered anyone. It kept the long
valley of the Wend, which rises from the Glass itself and runs west through
the fens and the grain country to the Ford and out of the realm. **The old
capital** stood where the Wend leaves the fens, a day's ride from the Hallow
and three from the Hearth. The kings'
duties ran in a loop: crowned at the Hearthstone, kept the Oath, protected
the chapter-house at the Glass, and buried in barrows on the Wend like the
lords before them.

Its institutions, almost all founded to keep names:

- **The temple of the Dawn**, with its shrines, chaplains, the cathedral in
  the capital, and the **book of returns** in every parish, used to read the
  wounded back into their shapes.
- **The border wardens**, sworn to the Line and not to the king. That detail
  would matter in O 612.
- **The Old Treaties (O 43)** with the hill clans: the clans keep their own
  names, gods and law; the kingdom keeps out of the hills; both keep the
  border together.
- **The sky legions (c. O 150)**, founded when a winged horse came down at the
  Hallow and chose a reed-cutter's daughter. Pegasi were held to be the
  Dawn's horses, the ones that once drew the morning. Their legions flew
  pickets over the whole valley, and from that height they learned the shape
  the realm draws from above ([Cosmology](01-cosmology.md#the-hallow-and-the-hearth)).
- **The war colleges (c. O 200)**, where officers learned strategy on a board
  of eight ranks and swore the Oath on graduation. Grandmasters beat the
  headmaster at the board.
- **The academy (O 263)** in the capital: the Old Tongue taught as grammar.
  Primers teach the fire-word at seven "and never said where the words come
  from", because the academy's own answer (from the Dawn) had become
  unfashionable by the sixth century.
- **The dueling halls**, which settled disputes over inheritance of names.
  In the old kingdom a disputed name went to trial by duel, and "a duelist
  is a question with a sharp answer".
- **The smith-saints**, iron-sworn smiths who struck makers' names into
  blades because iron does not forget.

### The Twins' War (O 331 – 338)

The eleventh king had twin sons, **Rhun and Rhodri**, born the same hour, and
the chronicle could not say who was elder. A king must spend his name at the
Hearthstone; the twins each insisted on being the one to pay. Their mother
had one sword forged for the two of them in the hope they would learn to
share. They did not. **Gemini** "learned to strike for both" instead, and
passed from brother to brother as the war turned.

The war ended on the great bridge of the old capital, where the brothers met
at last. A royal champion the chronicle does not name struck once to part
them. "Two men fell" (the Brave Sword). The chronicle is deliberately unclear
whether he meant to kill both. The crown passed to their sister, who went
down to the Hearthstone with a lantern and came back with no name. The border
chronicles call her **the Lantern Queen**, and it was her lamp the temple
copied for the **Vigil of Lamps**, the rite a chapel's Kindle still performs.

### Varen (O 404 – 471)

Varen was a quarry-smith's son at **the Cut**, the grey-stone quarries of the
border where the Hallow's stones were once cut to repair the ring. He took
the smith-saints' vow at twenty and spent nearly fifty years making plain iron
honest. He struck **VAREN** on the forte of every blade, beside the guard,
where a hand would feel it.

His rule, carved over the Cut's forge door and still there: **A MARK FOR
EVERY MAKER.** The mark is kept, not owned. Any smith trained in his line may
strike it, and has, for two centuries running. "Half the blades dug from this
ground still carry Varen's mark." Iron stamped with a name does not rust out
of true memory, and a Varen blade is said to *remember* its owners, which is
why grandfathers hand them down. (*"Stamped VAREN by the guard, same as your
father's. Same as his father's."*)

The Cut's forge is still lit. Its smith in S 34 is **Old Brannoc**, who taught
Edric to hold a sword ([05](05-dramatis-personae.md#old-brannoc)).

### The Storm-Year (O 519)

In spring the Hearth broke open: "where the mountain last broke open", a
watchtower stands. A year of storms followed. The peak clans came down from
their mountains for the first time in living memory, bringing an axe the
storms would not touch, **Stormbreaker**, and held the Hearth passes for the
king through the winter. It was the only time the clans fought for a banner,
and clan law forbade it again afterward: *never fly for a banner*.

The seers at the Glass noted that year, for the first time, that the corona
had thinned. The Sleeper was stirring. A king's name was not quite enough
anymore.

### The Waning (O 580 – 612)

**The Grey Winter (O 598–601).** Three winters came as one. The Grey Cough
followed the famine through every province. By the chaplains' books, a third
of the kingdom's names were lost in three years: the dead, and the forgotten
dead with no one left to keep them. Shrine bells rang on their own. The Glass
froze for the only time in history, and a fen carter's boy named **Hagen**
fell through the ice and came up three days later, talking about customers.

The corona dimmed so much that noon looked like evening.

**The Gentle King's second descent (O 601).** The king, crowned thirty years
before, did what no king had done: he went back down. He had no name left, so
he spent what the crown held in trust: the names of his house's dead from the
royal barrows, and the name of the kingdom's great lance, which the chronicle
names once and never again (**Doomblade**). The winter broke that spring.
The seers told him plainly that next time there would be nothing left to
spend but his people.

**The Marshal.** The hero of the Grey Winter was the kingdom's **Lord Marshal**,
a miller's son from the grain country who had risen from the ranks to first of his year at the war
college. He had spent three years moving grain through snow to villages that
were dying anyway. He loved the army and the army loved him. He had watched
his soldiers' names go into the chaplains' books by the thousand and decided
that the Oath was a luxury.

In O 603 a young academy mage named **Vantrell** brought him a stolen copy of
the **Middle Pages**, the rites of the Hearthstone. Read carefully, and
against the grain, they said something the kings had never used: the Sleeper
took any name, not only a king's, and a name given to it unwillingly gave
power back to whoever gave it. The Oath's fourth line (*what wakes it is not
suffered*) had been written against exactly this.

**The Great Vigil (O 611).** The Glass rose that autumn and the stones of the
Hallow leaned. The seers asked the king to call a **Great Vigil**: on one night
at midwinter, every subject in the kingdom would speak their own name aloud at
a shrine, all together, as the First Pages said. It was the oldest rite and
the slowest. The Marshal argued in council that it would not be enough, that
there was no time, and that the kingdom's gaols held enough condemned to buy
twenty years. The Gentle King refused and sent him to command the Hearth
garrison, in the hope that guarding the thing would teach him to fear it.

It did the opposite.

## The Unsworn Night (O 612 · S 0)

On the night before the Great Vigil, the Marshal took Vantrell and eleven
mages down the Hearth stair with a list.

At the top, in the Marshal's own hand, was **his own name**: the price of the
door, paid first. Under it was **the kingdom's name**, and under that the
names of the condemned from every gaol he could reach. The circle read them
into the dark around the Hearthstone. The
chronicle, written later by people who were not there, says the Sleeper's hum
rose until the stair shook, and that the Marshal knelt and listened to it for
a long time.

At midnight **every oath sworn by the kingdom's name broke at once.**

Knights woke unsworn. Garrison captains found they could not remember why
they obeyed. Wedding vows held, because they were sworn by the couple's own
names; the fealty of the realm did not. The Great Vigil never happened: on
the morning it should have, nobody could say the name they were meant to be
keeping.

The Marshal's regiments did not break, because he had spent ten years drilling
them. By dawn they held the **Hearth hold**, the old kings' fortress over the
coronation stair, and the road below it. At noon he stood on its wall with no
name and said that he was **the Emperor** and this was **the Empire**, a word
for a thing and not the name of one. **The Imperial Seat** dates from that
hour; the palace was built around the hold in the years after.

What his soldiers had instead of an oath was **drill**. The drills themselves
were the old kingdom's, the same forms the war colleges had always taught.
What was new was the **cadence** the drill-masters set them to, taken from the
sound in the Hearth: D and its shadow.
(*"The empire kept the drills and dropped the oaths."*)

## The Nine Days (S 0)

The Gentle King held the old capital in the valley. It was the only city in
the kingdom where people still kept faith with him by name. The empire took
nine days to take it.

- **The east gate held nine days.** Its captain was the queen's gate-warden,
  who carried the royal blade later called **Ragnarok**. "It held the gate
  when every wall behind it fell, and held it through the night": the ninth
  night. The *Last Bastion* scroll is her drill, written up afterward by one
  of her survivors.
- **The breach.** The circle wrote three copies of **Bolting** for the siege.
  Two opened the north wall. A young royal knight, **Bertil Stane**, held the
  breach with forty men.
- **The order.** On the eighth day the king sent the order to fall back to
  the river. His courier, **Jory Rusk**, rode to the Marshal's camp first and
  was paid to be late. He never delivered it, and he never threw it away: the
  sealed order stayed in his bag. Bertil Stane held the breach for a day and
  a half after the rest of the city was lost, "until the order to fall back
  rotted unread in a courier's bag." The empire took him alive, admired him,
  and made his stand its doctrine. He is **the Iron Wall**. Jory Rusk was given
  a black horse and the court circle's orders to carry. He was the first
  **Dark Rider**.
- **The river.** On the ninth night the Gentle King went down the Wend by boat
  with his son **Wystan**, the **Morning Crown** and a clay pot holding an
  ember from the palace hearth, lit, as every royal fire was, from the
  Hearthstone itself. He was dying of an arrow wound when they reached the
  Ford where the kingdom began. He asked to be carried into the river where
  the first king had stood. There he died, and the crown slipped from
  Wystan's hands into the water and was not found. The border says the Wend
  has run slow at the Ford ever since.

The old capital burned. Its name had already been spent with the kingdom's.
Grass grows now "where the old court once assembled".

## The Seat Ascendant (S 1 – 27)

### The Edict of the Roll (S 2)

The Emperor governed the way he had marched grain through the Grey Winter:
by list. **The Edict of the Roll** required every subject of the Empire to be
registered. Names were entered in the **Roll** in red ink and held "in trust
for the Seat", which in practice meant they could be called and spent. The
Edict also:

- **Numbered the provinces.** The grain provinces became the First to the
  Ninth. Mile markers count down to the Seat.
- **Required officers to surrender their names on commission.** An imperial
  officer is his rank. It is written in the Roll above his old name, which
  is struck through in red: *"Struck from the empire's roll of officers."*
  Every boss the warband faces is a title for this reason.
- **Imposed the curfew.** No lamp may burn after the dusk bell except by
  warrant. A lamp lit without one is a **rogue light**. As the corona dimmed,
  the curfew crept earlier: in the capital, the newest notices say *CURFEW AT
  NOON* over older ones that said *DUSK*.
- **Closed the free war colleges.** One remained, the **Imperial War College**
  at the Seat, teaching doctrine without the Oath. Its graduates still say
  "the war colleges" as if there were more than one.
- **Struck the fourth line** from the paladin oath, which had been copied from
  the Oath at the Ford. It is "generally hummed" now, by those who remember
  the tune.
- **Instituted the levy.** Each province owes the Seat bodies, and the Roll
  owes the Seat their names. Levy scrip pays them, when anything does.

### The Border Crown (S 2 – 28)

The west did not fall. The border wardens had sworn to **the Line**, not to
the kingdom's name, and the banner-houses of the Marches swore to each other,
by their own names, around a fire. When the Oath broke everywhere else, the
Marches found they were still bound.

**Wystan**, the Gentle King's son, became **the Uncrowned**. He could not be
crowned, because the crown was in the river and the Hearthstone was in the
Emperor's hands, so he kept his name and refused the title. He held court at
**Wendhall**, a border hall on the river above the Ford, and kept the ember
from the Hearth burning in its great fireplace. For twenty-six years the
**Border Crown** was the last place where the Oath at the Ford was said
whole: wardens, the Ford College, a few sky-legion pickets who would not fly
for a word, the smith at the Cut, and yeomen who owed "the crown a quiver
this season".

### The Standard Wars (S 3 – 19)

The Empire fed on its neighbors first. In sixteen years it took the standards
of three realms and spent their names:

- **The Saltmarch**, the salt-cities on the southern coast. Now the Tenth,
  Eleventh and Twelfth Provinces.
- **The Tarn**, a lake-kingdom in the northern lowlands. Now the Thirteenth.
- **The Dry Country**, beyond the Hearth to the east, whose people kept
  names in knots of cord. Now the Fourteenth. Its knot-archives were read
  into the dark in a single winter.

Their standards hang in the Seat, "stacked like trophies", on the armor of the
Emperor's last guardians.

### The Defacing (S 9)

The temple kept names: the book of returns in every parish, the naming
registers in every shrine. The Empire wanted them. In S 9 imperial clerks
seized the shrine books province by province and carried them to the Seat to
be read at the Hearthstone. Statues of the Dawn had their faces chipped
away, because a face is how a name is recognized. The bishops went to the
Seat and were given the cathedral there. The **village chaplains** and the
**fen priests** did not go, and the temple has been two temples since.

People began naming children in whispers, or not at all.

### The Taking (S 12)

The Emperor had no seers. The High Commission, founded S 5, was a bureau of
hedge-seers who scried in shards of black glass from the Hearth caldera and
saw very little. The circle wanted the Thread.

In S 12 the court circle came to the chapter-house at the Glass with the
levy behind it and took the novices down to the water, meaning to send a
seer through to the far side and bring back sight from underneath. None of
them came home but one. **Tamlin** came back without his name and with his
eyes open, and he could see every future that ended. The chroniclers who
know more than that do not write it down.

The chapter-house's carter went into the water after them. It was **Hagen**,
drowning for the second time. He came up on the far side, on a landing of the
stair under the Hearth, with a small boy by the collar, and walked out of the
mountain two days later. He has been ahead of everyone since.

The court circle's records list Tamlin as "the first success" and "the first
casualty". Both are true.

### The rest of the Seat's rise

- **S 13. The academy burns.** Vantrell, by now **the Archmage**, takes its
  library into the court circle's vault, the "locked door" the Sage recruits
  talk about, and lets the rest burn. The mages it trained scatter.
- **S 15. The Doctrine of Lances.** **Leofric**, an old-kingdom knight who
  found faith in the Emperor after the Unsworn Night, writes the cavalry
  doctrine the Imperial War College still teaches. He is **the Knight
  Commander**.
- **S 15. The Berserker King.** **Wat**, a Grey Winter orphan who drove carts
  on the Roads and was pressed into the Marshal's baggage train at the Nine
  Days, has spent ten years teaching deserters to forget their names
  in battle so that they cannot be afraid. He calls it **the red**. His
  school's pupils crown him at the fens' edge by an acclaim nobody dares
  withhold.
- **S 16. The Lieutenant.** Tamlin, eleven, raised since the Taking in the
  circle's keep on the fen causeway, warns the Emperor of a plot nobody else
  had seen. The plotters are fed. The Emperor names him **Lieutenant**, the
  one who stands in his place in sight. **Tiernan Vale**, the dueling halls'
  seventh *proof*, is assigned to guard and train him. Within two years the
  boy is choosing the Blade Lord's duels, and Tiernan calls him *master*.
- **S 21.** The High Commission is disbanded, redundant beside the Lieutenant.
  Its sky-legion escorts are left without orders. Some fly west to the Border
  Crown, which gives them the far pickets of the Marches.
- **S 25. The Great Feeding.** The Archmage's reading of the Middle Pages
  promises that when the Sleeper has swallowed enough it will "turn toward
  the hand that fed it". The Emperor believes that when it turns, he will hold
  a world in which nothing comes next: an empire without an after, without
  rebellion and without end. To reach that sum the Seat needs the two
  largest reservoirs of names left. One is **the Hallow**, where the
  dragons' first names lie. The other is **the Marches**. The Lieutenant
  leaves the keep and goes below, to the landing on the far side of the Glass,
  to watch the feeding from where it happens. He has lived there since.

## The Second Push (S 28 – 30)

The Marches straddle the lower Wend. Wendhall stands on the north bank a
day's ride upriver from the Ford; the Ford is the only crossing the Marches
could hold, and the old kingdom's roads from the east all came along the
north bank, under Wendhall's eye. The empire came the other way.

- **Spring S 28.** The border clans break the Old Treaties. Their warchief,
  **Skarde**, accepts the empire's new terms, opens the hill roads south of
  the river to imperial columns, and raids the north bank from the hills.
  Wendhall has to hold two fronts.
- **Summer S 28. The Siege of the Ford.** The **Knight Commander** comes
  through the hill country and arrives at the Ford from the south. The
  warden-captain at Oathford, **Anders Carrow**, holds the crossing for
  eleven weeks waiting for relief from Wendhall that cannot come. When
  his wardens are eating their boots, he surrenders on terms. His soldiers
  keep their lives and stay on the border, exempt from the levy. The price is
  his name, entered in the Roll that afternoon. He is made **Warden of the
  March**, the **Iron Captain**. His first order closes the crossing. He
  signs it with an iron stamp because he has nothing else to sign with.
- **Autumn S 28. The border burns.** The Push crosses at the Ford and
  reaches Wendhall. Its gate is held by a young sentinel named **Cael**, posted
  there three years earlier by Wystan himself with orders to hold it until
  relieved. The gate never opens; the lances come over the east wall. Wystan
  dies in his hall. His son **Edric**, sixteen, is carried out the back with
  the banner and the ember pot by the smith **Brannoc**. The warden village
  of **Birchwick** burns the same week with the families of the wardens inside
  it. **Voss** comes home from patrol to his wife's and daughter's names and
  nothing else. The Ford College closes the day before its Grandmaster
  board, which **Kira** was to have sat. Its headmaster dies in the Push that
  week.
- **Winter S 28.** The gate of Wendhall is a gate to a burned hall. Cael holds
  it. He is still holding it five years later.

### The Glass rises (S 29 – 30)

The Great Feeding reaches the fens. The circle reads the Hallow's first names
into the dark and the ring of stones begins to wake in the wrong order. The
Glass goes black and rises into the reeds. The dead of the drowned villages
stand up. The fens become the corruption's country.

At the chapter-house a novice of fifteen, **Sera** (in the roll, **Serafen**
**(III)**), looks into the Glass and sees the Thread: every road of the coming years at once, and on
one of them a young man walking at the head of a column and counting the
names behind him. At the same moment, on the far side of the Glass, the
Lieutenant looks up and sees her seeing. They have been watching each other
ever since, without ever having met.

In S 30 the circle comes back. The last Keeper of the order graduates her
early, a ceremony of one sentence, and gives her the **Last Pages** and the
**novice roll** with Tamlin's name still in it. She is sent west to find "the
one who counts". A sky lancer flying a picket nobody had ordered, **Astrid**,
finds her walking on the causeway and carries her out.

The Seat declares the Marches pacified.

## The Hiding (S 28 – 33)

Edric lives as a fugitive in the hills above the Ford with a banner he
cannot fly. Sera finds him there in S 31 because she has already seen where
he will be. The warband gathers slowly:

- **Voss**, who has been killing imperial patrols alone, keeping a ledger.
- **Kira**, who arrives with a cart of confiscated imperial ledgers and a
  proof that the Roll is shrinking faster than the dead can explain.
- **Rowan**, who rides west from the Sixth Province with nine deserters and
  a lance a ruined temple blessed.
- **Astrid**, who has stayed near Sera since the causeway without being asked.
- **Cael**, still at the gate. Edric goes to Wendhall to bury his father's
  hearthstone. The sentinel at the gate asks for the watchword. Edric doesn't
  know it, so Cael follows him to find out.

Around them gather the people any war collects: deserters, quarrymen, sellswords
owed scrip, a dancer who danced for a garrison that never came home, a thief
who stole a magistrate's signet.

## The March (S 34)

On the night before, they sit around the ember from the Hearth, rekindled in
a fire pit by the Ford, and Sera tells them she can see a path: fragmented,
but there.
