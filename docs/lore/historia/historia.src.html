I
Chapter one

The World

Before anything happened there was a stillness with no after in it. It is still there, under the land. Everything in this book is about what has been done, for thousands of years, to keep it asleep.

The Stillness

The liturgy's first page: Before her, nothing came next. That condition had weight and extent. It is the world as it is when nothing happens to it, and it lies beneath the land the way bedrock lies beneath soil. It is not a creature and not a god. When it stirs, the world near it loses its after: water stops flowing, the dead stop staying down, futures stop branching. People call that the corruption.

Common folk call it the Sleeper. The court circle's clerks call it the Entity. The liturgy leaves a blank where its name would go, because nobody knows what naming it would do.

The Dawn

Something answered the Stillness, and the answer was the first morning. The faith calls her the Dawn; seers call her the Weaver; imperial clerks, when they must, say the Spent.

She laid time over the Stillness like weft across warp, so that one thing could follow another. She named the dragons first, then fire and wind and thunder, then rivers and people. Her words are the Old Tongue. A mage saying the fire-word, or a dragon breathing, is speaking it.

The Spending

The Sleeper turned over for the first time. The cloth tore, mountains opened, and new-named things began to forget themselves. She could not fight the ground her cloth lay on, so she did the one thing a name can do against something that has none. She spent it.

She knelt in the lowland where the fens are now and paid her name out into the world, down to the last syllable. The ground opened where she knelt and filled with black, perfectly still water: the Glass. Her first-named, the dragons, lay down around it, and over the ages their spines became the standing stones of the Hallow. That night the stars seemed to fall. Folk remember it as the Starfall. The next morning the sun rose hollow.

Every name anyone has carried since is part of her change.

A spent coin does not vanish; it goes into other hands. A child named at a roadside shrine is given a little of it. A river that people keep calling by its name keeps its share. While the world is full of names that are held and spoken, it is full of after, and the Sleeper stays asleep. The seers enjoyed the pun: the Sleeper is the world with no change in it.

Names the physics of the world

This is why the game has so few proper nouns. It is a world running out of names. The old kingdom is "the old kingdom" because its name was spent. Every boss is only a title because imperial officers surrender their names on commission. Proper names grow commoner the farther you are from the Sleeper: the border still names its rivers, and the Seat names nothing.

Kept

Held, spoken, remembered. Shrines keep children's names for a copper. Wardens keep ledgers of the dead. Smiths strike names into iron. Soldiers give each other epithets, and an epithet makes a name heavier and harder to take.

Spent

Given up by its holder, to buy something. The Dawn bought sleep. The old kings bought quiet at the Hearthstone. The berserk school's pupils buy fearlessness, and forget themselves.

Fed

Taken from someone else and given to the Sleeper. It gives power back to whoever feeds it. This is the empire's whole art, and the one thing the Oath at the Ford forbade.

Unmade

Thinned by the Sleeper's nearness. Prayer boards go blank; mile markers wear smooth; the seers' notes stop mid-sentence.

An oath is sworn by a name. If the name goes, the oath breaks. That is what happened on the Unsworn Night, and it is why the empire runs on drill: The empire kept the drills and dropped the oaths.

The Hollow Sun and the Eclipse

The sun has been hollow since the Starfall: a black disc where her name used to shine, and a thin gold corona. The corona is the light of every name still held, seen from far away. Border folk call it the rim; old books call it the sun's crown, which is why the kings wore gold.

When names thin, the corona thins. The land has lived in a long dusk since the empire began feeding it. Only the Hearth and the Deep have true night.

The Eclipse, the ink disc crossing the sun during a run, is the court circle at work LEARNED. While the warband is in the field, cantors at the Hearthstone read names from the imperial Roll into the dark, around the clock. The disc is the Roll's ink. An officer's name flares back into the corona when he dies. Kindling a chapel is the Vigil of Lamps: a lamp lit and a book of names read aloud.

The Hallow and the Hearth

The Sleeper is vast, and the whole old kingdom lies on it. It comes nearest the surface in two places. At the Hallow, in the fens, it breathes: the water rises and falls with it. Under the Hearth, a mountain to the east whose stone is warm all year, its heart beats. The old kings found a hall there, the Hearthstone, and each went down once, at his crowning, to spend his name.

The two openings meet underground. Whoever goes down through the Glass and lives comes up on a landing of the old kings' coronation stair, beneath the mountain LEARNED. The Lieutenant lives there. From high enough, only the sky legions ever flew that high, the rivers, fens and ridges draw the outline of something asleep on its side. The empire disbanded the legions and kept their charts.

The Thread

One thing of hers was not spent: her sight along the weave. It stayed where she knelt, in the Glass. The first seers found it and called it the Thread. One seer holds it at a time. Sera holds it now, and she is the last.

A run is one thread of the March of the year 34 of the Seat. If the commander falls, the thread is cut, and Sera begins again from the one point her sight can always reach: the night before the March, at the fire by the Ford. That fire is home base. It burns from the last ember of the old kings' Hearth.

A cut thread falls into the Sleeper, which keeps everything. Its memories seep upward, and they seep most into the people nearest it:

SoldiersDéjà vu, rarely. Why do I always remember the crow?
Border bossesDreams. From a dream where I died.
Imperial officersNightmares they call faith, or, for the Archmage, data.
The LieutenantEvery cut thread, from underneath.
HagenEverything. He finds it good for business.

One thread that holds does not hold the Sleeper. A cloth does. Every victory Sera weaves is a thread laid into it.

Magic three things done to names

Anima says

Speak fire's Old Tongue name and fire remembers it is fire, here. Learned at the academy. Read the words aloud and the fire follows.

Light calls

Healing reminds a body of its own shape. Revival reads a dying soldier's name back from the parish book until it holds. Breathe. Now remember who you are.

Dark unsays

Takes a piece of someone's name and keeps it. Taught by the court circle from the stolen Middle Pages.

Weapon arts cost blood because blood carries a name the way ink carries a word: an art spends a little of the wielder for a moment of more.

The night before the Vigil, the Marshal goes down the Hearth stair with twelve mages and a list: his own name first, then the kingdom's, then the condemned. The siege of the old capital. The east gate holds nine days. A young knight, Bertil Stane, holds the Breach with forty men, waiting for an order to fall back that the courier was paid to be late with. The Gentle King dies in the river at the Ford, and the crown goes into the water.

Shrine books seized. The Dawn's statues have their faces chipped away. The temple becomes two temples.

S 12

The court circle takes the seer novices to the Glass. One comes home, with no name and eyes that see every ending. Hagen goes into the water after them and drowns for the second time.

S 13 · 15 · 16

The academy burns. The Knight Commander writes the Doctrine of Lances. The Berserker King is crowned by an acclaim nobody dares withhold. An eleven-year-old boy is named the Emperor's Lieutenant, and a perfect duelist is assigned to guard him.

S 25

The Archmage promises that when the Sleeper has swallowed enough, it will turn toward the hand that fed it. The Emperor wants a world where nothing comes next. He needs two last reservoirs of names: the dragons of the Hallow, and the Marches.

The hill clans break the Old Treaties and open the hill roads. The warden-captain at the Ford holds eleven weeks and surrenders on terms, paying with his name. The border burns. Wendhall falls, its gate still shut.

The first dragon wakes in the wrong order. A fifteen-year-old novice looks into the Glass and sees every road of the next five years, and on one of them a young man counting the names behind him. On the far side, the Lieutenant sees her seeing.

S 34

They sit around the ember, rekindled in a fire pit above the Ford, and Sera tells them she can see a path: fragmented, but there. Every run begins here.

Chapter three

The Realm

The Wend rises from the Glass and runs west, through the fens and the grain country, past the Ford and out of the realm. The March goes east, upstream. Soldiers call it walking the river back to where it's dirty.

The realm, west to east

Seen from high enough, the fens, the river and the ridges trace something curled up asleep, with the Glass at its mouth and the Hearth at its heart.

Act IEverything named: rivers, fords, smiths, people.

Act IVNothing named. The heraldry office is ash.

The name gradient. The world gets darker as the March goes east because it gets more nameless, and it gets more nameless because it is nearer the thing that eats names.

IV
Chapter four

Powers

Everyone who holds power here does one thing to names. Know which, and the faction writes itself.

FEEDS

The Empire

What is, remains.

The old kingdom's own people and three conquered neighbors, ruled from the Seat by a man with no name through a register of everyone else's. The Roll, the levy, the curfew, numbered provinces, and a drill cadence that empties soldiers a little each year. Not a hive of villains: a tired country that got used to something.

FEEDS

The Court Circle

Every name is a sum.

The mages who went down the stair on the Unsworn Night. Twelve at a time in a circle around the Hearthstone, reading the Roll into the dark. They hold the stolen Middle Pages, study the loop as "rifts", and still believe the Sleeper can be steered.

KEEPS

The Old Kingdom

The strong serve the weak.

Six hundred years of nameless kings, founded on the Oath at the Ford: every name holds the dark down equally, and the crown is a debt the king pays first. Its name was spent. What survives is its roads, its horn call, its teal, an ember from its hearth, and its heir.

KEEPS

The Temple of the Dawn

Once more, and then once more after that.

Shrines name children and keep the names. Parishes keep the book of returns. Since the Defacing it is two temples: bishops in the Seat blessing the levy, and village chaplains and fen priests who hid their books.

KEEPS

The Seer Order

Hold on to the thread.

Keepers of the Thread and the Hallow, fenfolk in cream, older than the kingdom. The Last Pages of the liturgy, what sleeps below, what wakes it, and the thread by which the world is pulled back, were theirs. Raided in S 12, burned in S 30. Sera is what is left.

KEEPS

Border Wardens

Still standing.

Rangers sworn to the Line, not to any name, so their oath survived the Unsworn Night and the Marches held for twenty-six more years. Every warden keeps two ledgers: patrol routes, and the dead. Writing a name in the second keeps it out of the dark.

KEEPS

Smith-saints

A mark for every maker.

Iron-sworn smiths who strike names into blades, because iron does not forget. They cursed the Blood Forge, which quenches an edge in the smith's own name. It works anyway.

SPENDS

The Berserk School

Forget, and fear goes with it.

Wat's school at the fens' edge teaches the red: forget your name in battle and you cannot be afraid. The best pupils never quite remember again, and are chained at night. The corruption finds nothing in them to change.

KEEPS

The Sky Legions

The mount picks the rider.

Pegasi are held to be the Dawn's horses, and they choose their riders. From their pickets the legions learned the shape of the land. The empire folded them into a courier service and never reissued their orders.

KEEPS

The Clans

We were here first.

Two peoples. The hill clans keep their dead in blood-song, and broke the Old Treaties in S 28. The peak clans ride wyverns, crown no kings, settle disputes by a wyvern lord's shadow, and voted to stay out of this war. Some riders lost the vote and came anyway.

KEEPS

Guilds and Troupes

Checkpoints cannot tax a song.

A guild-contracted sellsword is under contract, not under the Roll, which is the only legal way to keep your name out of it. Bards smuggle the old songs, and the names in them, past every gate.

KEEPS

The Partisans

Still here.

No leader, so nobody to feed. Numbers scratched off milestones, a two-note whistle from the next hill, a cellar door that opens on the third knock.

The March

The Titled

Imperial officers surrender their names to the Roll on commission and answer to rank. Every boss is a title for this reason. Under each title was a person. Some names come back when they fall. Some come back only one way.

The price was his name. He eats with his troops, mends his own banner, and signs his orders with an iron stamp. His music keeps returning to D: duty, not ambition.

VII
Chapter seven

Bestiary & Relics

What the warband fights and why it is like that, and the history behind the arms that turn up in the loot.

The levy

Farmhands and quarrymen entered in the Roll: LEVY BY ORDER. AGAIN. They march to the imperial cadence and get quieter every year. They carry no light. A deserter who turns his uniform inside out and is called by name starts to glow again.

Officers and affixes

Rank comes with gifts from the circle: pieces of other people's names read into an officer so he is more than one man. Wounds closing on someone else's shape. A ward bought with a village. Where affixes show, the Lieutenant has tampered with the weave.

Hunters and the unrolled

Hunters close on anyone whose name has got loud enough to collect. Village raiders are the unrolled: survivors of a fed village who came home to houses nobody remembers, and now take from villages that still have names.

Zombies

The fens' dead, whose names were fed. Nothing calls them home, so they wander. Burn the dead, or give them to the light. Light works by calling a name back; if there is none, burn.

Revenants

Zombies the fens have kept long enough. They rebuild a shape from the names of the living, a little at a time, and stop shambling. What fills behind their ribs when they wound you is your name.

Dragons, the woken

The Dawn's first-named, asleep as the stones of the Hallow. The circle is reading their names into the dark, and each wakes without one, in the wrong order. They go for the warband because named things smell like what they lost. Their breath is the Old Tongue.

The Dragon Lord

The first thing the Dawn ever named. It regards the war the way a mountain regards weather. Not the warband's enemy. In the way.

Wyverns

Hatched in the peak clans' nests in winters when the Hallow's stones stir, from what the dragons dream. Near the fens they won't eat: they can smell the dreams they came from going wrong.

IX
Chapter nine

Sealed

True, and never said in the game. The game only points at these: a rhyme nobody believes, a bell, a violin. Break a seal to read it.

Every name anyone carries is part of the Dawn's change. She is not gone; she is asleep in every name. That makes two sleepers: the Stillness below and the Dawn within. When names thin, the one below stirs. When names are said together, the one within begins to wake. Edric's Power stolen from a sleeping god is truer than he knows: the Emperor has been feeding on her, one name at a time.

It has swallowed so much of her that it has begun to hum her note, D, with its own shadow beneath. It is trying to say the name it ate. The empire's drill is the same interval, set to marching.

Wounding the Sleeper opens it, and everything it swallowed pours out: the levy, the conquered provinces, the burned villages, the Lieutenant's name, the old kingdom's own name, thirty-four years of the Roll. The choir Act I never had is them. They sing Ember Dusk, the song of the road you walked, because it is the first thing they hear. The complete Thread arrives only when everyone sings it: her name can only be said by everyone who carries her change, at once. For one chord the world says it, and the Sleeper, emptied, sinks back into true sleep. A dawn with no god behind it. The empire would call it a rogue dawn.

Every thread she re-weaves costs her some of her own name. At the chapter-house she was Serafen in the roll and Sera to everyone. The long form went first; by now even she struggles to remember there was more. She has told nobody. What she fears is not dying. It is finishing the March as someone nobody can call. Hagen remembers the long form. The one time he says it, it frightens her.

He drowned in the Glass twice and came back both times. He walks threads sideways, and he has walked more of them than Sera. The second time, in S 12, he came up on the landing under the Hearth with a small boy by the collar. The circle's records credit themselves with the Lieutenant. Hagen brought him back. He hums the Dawn-office off-key because he is humming the note that isn't in it.

The shrine bells ring on their own out here. Twice, then silence. Then twice. The chapter-house bells are ringing a two-syllable name, calling home the one novice who came back without it: Tam-lin. He has never let himself hear them.

The Gentle King's order to fall back, sealed with a crown, never delivered. The first rider was paid to be late with it and never threw it away. His son has carried it since. It is the order the Iron Wall has been waiting for since the Breach.

It is the word for what Edric carried out of the burning hall in a clay pot, the fire the warband sits around every night before the March. Cael will not say it. Neither will this book.
